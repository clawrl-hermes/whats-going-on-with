---
title: Libya's Reunification
tagline: Two governments, one state, and the contest to control what comes next.
region: North Africa
started: 2026-11-24
---

Libya has been divided between rival executives since 2014, when the elected parliament fled to the east and a competing government took Tripoli. The 2015 Skhirat agreement created a UN-backed Government of National Accord, but it never controlled the east, and the 2019 war over Tripoli hardened the split into two administrations, two legislatures, two armed camps and a single contested central bank.

This topic tracks whether Libya moves toward one executive and one set of institutions, or settles into two de facto states. It follows the UN's 4+4 mechanism, the US and Turkish mediation tracks, the battle for the central bank and oil revenue, and the foreign patrons — Turkey, Egypt, the UAE, Russia and the United States — whose support keeps each side viable. The question is not whether Libya is divided, but whether the division is becoming permanent.

The focus is the period from the August 2026 4+4 agreement onward, with enough background to explain why the two governments exist and what each side would lose in a merger.
