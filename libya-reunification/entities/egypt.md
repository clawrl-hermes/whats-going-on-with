# Egypt

Egypt is the eastern camp's oldest and most consistent foreign supporter, providing arms, training, air support and diplomatic cover to Khalifa Haftar since 2014. Its priorities are a friendly government in Benghazi, control of the border and water security, and preventing Islamist groups from gaining a foothold. Since late 2026 it has also courted Tripoli, a hedge against Turkey's growing role.

## Key Actions

- **2014-2019:** Supplied Haftar's forces and backed Operation Dignity, then supported the 2019 Tripoli offensive with arms and logistics.
- **2020:** Opposed Turkey's military intervention and warned that it would defend its interests in the eastern Mediterranean.
- **7 November 2026:** Reopened its embassy in Tripoli after 12 years, sending Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty to press for energy projects and voice objections to the Turkey maritime memorandum.
- **13 November 2026:** Intelligence chief Hassan Rashad met Saddam Haftar in Cairo, keeping a separate channel to the east.
- **19 November 2026:** President Sisi and Dbeibah signed energy, fisheries and reconstruction agreements in Cairo.

## Current Position

Cairo is running two policies at once: a security partnership with Haftar and a commercial and diplomatic opening to Dbeibah. The energy agenda — gas pipelines, refining Libyan crude in Egypt and an electricity link — is designed to make Egypt indispensable to whichever government emerges, while the embassy reopening gives Cairo a presence in Tripoli it has not had since 2012. It wants a unified Libya that is stable, federal enough to protect eastern autonomy, and free of a Turkey-aligned government.

## Constraints

- Its influence depends on Haftar, whose alignment with Turkey and the UAE limits Cairo's leverage.
- It disagrees with Abu Dhabi over Sudan, complicating a common front on Libya.
- Egypt's own economic crisis limits the finance it can offer reconstruction.
