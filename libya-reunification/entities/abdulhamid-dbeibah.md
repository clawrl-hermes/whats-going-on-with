# Abdulhamid Dbeibah

Dbeibah leads the internationally recognised Government of National Unity, the executive created by the 2021 Libyan Political Dialogue Forum. His authority rests on holding Tripoli, the central bank and the diplomatic recognition that comes with both, not on an electoral mandate. He has refused to hand power to any rival cabinet since 2022 and is central to every proposal for a unified executive.

## Key Actions

- **February 2021:** Selected by the Libyan Political Dialogue Forum to head a unity government with a mandate to prepare elections.
- **2022:** Refused to hand over to the House of Representatives' appointed prime minister, Fathi Bashagha, and retained control of Tripoli after armed clashes.
- **April 2026:** Signed the unified budget framework with eastern authorities, the first shared financial arrangement in 13 years.
- **November 2026:** Held talks with US envoy Massad Boulos on the reunification plan, which would leave him as prime minister of a merged executive.
- **19 November 2026:** Signed energy, refining and fisheries agreements with Egypt in Cairo, bypassing the eastern administration.

## Current Position

Dbeibah is the weakest of the two camp leaders militarily but the strongest institutionally. He controls the capital, the internationally recognised ministries and, through the Central Bank, the revenue account that pays salaries across both halves of the country. The US plan makes him the continuity candidate, which has turned him from a transitional figure into the main obstacle to a clean reset.

## Constraints

- He has no electoral mandate and his government's legitimacy is rejected by the House of Representatives.
- His control of Tripoli depends on armed groups that would resist a Haftar-led security arrangement.
- The central bank crisis and the dinar's slide erode the patronage that keeps his coalition together.
