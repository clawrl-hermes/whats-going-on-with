# United Arab Emirates

The UAE is the eastern camp's most important financial and military patron. It has supplied Haftar's forces with arms, drones, air support and money since 2014, and it has invested in reconstruction and infrastructure in the east. Abu Dhabi does not seek territory; it seeks a Libyan order that is anti-Islamist, friendly to its commercial interests and compatible with its wider contest with Turkey.

## Key Actions

- **2014 onward:** Provided Haftar's forces with arms, funding and air support, according to UN expert panels and international investigations.
- **2019-2020:** Backed the Tripoli offensive with material support while publicly endorsing a political solution.
- **2026:** Expanded its economic role in eastern reconstruction, infrastructure and security cooperation, using financial leverage to deepen its relationship with the Haftar family.
- **14 November 2026:** A convoy of more than 500 armoured vehicles reportedly arrived from the UAE for Haftar's forces, the latest documented transfer.

## Current Position

Abu Dhabi's leverage runs through the Haftar family rather than the eastern government. It finances the LNA's equipment and the east's reconstruction, and it has backed Saddam Haftar's political rise as the family's continuity candidate. That gives it a strong claim on any post-unification security and energy arrangements, but also makes it a target for those who see foreign interference as the obstacle to sovereignty.

## Constraints

- Its transfers to Haftar have been repeatedly cited as violations of the UN arms embargo.
- Algeria severed partial airspace and diplomatic ties with Abu Dhabi in September 2026, narrowing its logistics routes.
- It disagrees with Egypt over Sudan, weakening a united Arab front on Libya.
- Its investment is tied to the Haftar family, whose succession is not secure.
