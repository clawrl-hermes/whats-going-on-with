# Turkey

Turkey is the west's indispensable external patron. Its military intervention in 2020 stopped Haftar's offensive on Tripoli, and its troops, drones and trainers remain the backbone of the capital's defence. Ankara's leverage now runs in both directions: it has a maritime agreement with the east and a security relationship with the west, and neither camp can settle without it.

## Key Actions

- **November 2019:** Signed a maritime delimitation memorandum with the Tripoli government, claiming an exclusive economic zone that overlaps Greek and Egyptian claims.
- **2020:** Intervened militarily to halt Haftar's Tripoli offensive, securing the GNA and its own base access.
- **October 2026:** The eastern House of Representatives ratified the maritime memorandum after speaker Aguila Saleh was removed, extending Ankara's reach to the Benghazi camp.
- **October 2026:** Hosted east-west military unification talks in Turkey, presenting itself as a broker between the two commands.
- **2026:** Expanded trade and defence cooperation with Tripoli, with both sides citing a $5 billion bilateral trade target.

## Current Position

Turkey is the only foreign state with forces inside Libya and the only one whose security guarantee the western armed groups trust. It has converted that position into influence over both camps: the east has accepted its maritime claim, and the west still depends on its air defence and training. Ankara's priority is to protect the maritime deal and its economic stake in reconstruction, which makes it a necessary party to any unified executive.

## Constraints

- Its maritime claim is rejected by Greece, Egypt and Cyprus, and the US called the eastern ratification "provocative".
- A permanent military presence is unpopular with many Libyans and would be a bargaining chip in any unification deal.
- Its economy and currency make an open-ended deployment expensive.
