Libya remains split between the Tripoli-based Government of National Unity under Abdulhamid Dbeibah and the Benghazi-based Government of National Stability under Osama Hamad, with the House of Representatives and Khalifa Haftar's Libyan National Army backing the east. The UN-facilitated 4+4 agreement of 30 August created a route to a single executive and elections within 24 months, but the High Council of State rejects its outcomes and no election date or unified cabinet exists. A parallel US and Turkish track would keep Dbeibah as prime minister and install Saddam Haftar at the head of the Presidential Council, a family power-sharing arrangement that has not been signed. The binding constraint is financial: a contested central bank succession, a dinar near 11 to the dollar and the loss of 365,000 barrels a day to pipeline closures are eroding the revenue any unity government would need.

## The Open Question

Can the 4+4 framework produce a single executive before the central bank crisis and oil shutdowns make partition the cheaper outcome for both camps?
