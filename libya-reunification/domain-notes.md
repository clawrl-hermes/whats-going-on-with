# Libya's Reunification — Structural Notes

> Recent developments are tracked in `timeline/libya-reunification-timeline.md` at month resolution. This file records structural context, recurring dynamics and the indicators to watch, not a chronology.

## The Core Dynamic

- Libya is a single state with two executives, two legislatures and two armed forces. The split is institutional and financial, not a fixed front line: the western government holds international recognition and the Central Bank, while the eastern government holds most oil terminals and the strongest army.
- Neither side can defeat the other or govern alone. That stalemate produces repeated unity deals that distribute offices without merging institutions.
- The 2026 shift is that the two main camps — Dbeibah's government and Haftar's command — now negotiate directly, while the bodies that gave the old order its legitimacy, the High Council of State and the eastern administration, are being bypassed.

## The Two Administrations

- **West:** Government of National Unity (GNU) under Abdulhamid Dbeibah, based in Tripoli. Recognised by the UN and most states; controls the Central Bank, the official exchange rate and the diplomatic apparatus.
- **East:** Government of National Stability (GNS) under Osama Hamad, based in Benghazi. Appointed by the House of Representatives in 2022, backed by the Libyan National Army (LNA) and Khalifa Haftar; administers the east and most southern cities.
- **Legislatures:** the House of Representatives (HoR) in the east, elected in 2014, and the High Council of State (HCS) in Tripoli, an appointed consultative body created by the 2015 Skhirat agreement.
- **Military:** the LNA under Haftar, and a coalition of western armed groups nominally under the GNU. Neither is a unified national army.

## The 4+4 Mechanism

- Four representatives of the GNU and four from the LNA general command, facilitated by UNSMIL. Talks began in April 2026; the agreement was signed on 30 August 2026.
- It agreed to reconstitute the High National Elections Commission, revise the electoral framework and hold presidential and parliamentary elections within 24 months under one executive.
- The HoR endorsed it on 14 September; the HCS rejected it on 5 October as procedurally and constitutionally flawed. No elections board has been seated and no date set.
- It is a leader-to-leader format: it includes the actors who can block a settlement and excludes those who can dispute its legitimacy.

## The Boulos Plan

- US mediation track led by Massad Boulos, President Trump's senior adviser for Arab and African affairs.
- Proposes Dbeibah remaining prime minister and Saddam Haftar, Khalifa Haftar's son, becoming head of the Presidential Council.
- Turkey's leverage comes from its military presence in western Libya and its maritime memorandum, ratified by the eastern HoR on 20 October 2026 after speaker Aguila Saleh was removed.
- The plan is an elite bargain, not an institutional merger; it has not been signed or published as a formal text.

## The Economy

- Hydrocarbons account for more than 60% of GDP and about 90% of exports and government revenue, according to the World Bank. Oil output averaged about 1.3 million barrels a day in 2025, with 1.35 million projected for 2026.
- GDP was $48.1 billion in 2025.
- The Central Bank of Libya holds oil revenue and sets the official exchange rate (about 6.6 dinars to the dollar). The parallel rate reached about 10.70-11 in November 2026.
- The Libyan Investment Authority holds assets above $80 billion, much of it frozen abroad.
- The April 2026 unified budget framework was the first shared financial arrangement in 13 years. Public debt is estimated above 300 billion dinars ($46.5 billion).
- The Central Bank succession dispute — Fawzi Abokhzam's appointment and a US Federal Reserve pause on interactions — is the most acute financial risk.

## Security and Foreign Patrons

- **Turkey:** military presence in the west, supports the GNU and the maritime memorandum; now also courted by the east. Holds a practical veto over any security arrangement.
- **Egypt:** backs Haftar and the east, but runs parallel channels to Tripoli; opposes the Turkey maritime deal and hosts both camps' intelligence and military officials.
- **UAE:** financial and military support to Haftar; backs the Boulos plan according to Libyan analysts.
- **Russia:** Africa Corps presence in the east, logistics through Tobruk and Benghazi; shifting supply routes after Ukrainian strikes on Russian vessels.
- **United States:** leads the Boulos track, wants a quick unified executive and has threatened sanctions against obstructors.
- **UN:** UNSMIL under Hanna Tetteh, mandate renewed to 31 October 2027; no enforcement capacity.

## Dedup Pitfalls

- The "Boulos plan," the "US initiative," the "reunification plan" and the "Dbeibah-Saddam deal" usually refer to the same proposal. Do not add each as a separate development.
- The 4+4 agreement is often described as the "Smaller Convening" agreement; they are the same 30 August text.
- The GNU and the GNS are both called "the government" in Libyan reporting. Always specify which one.
- Eastern and western outlets report the same cabinet meeting or decree as rival initiatives; check whether the event is a statement, a decree or an implementation measure.
- Casualty and oil-loss figures from Libyan sources are frequently revised; keep the NOC figure and the outlet's own estimate separate.
