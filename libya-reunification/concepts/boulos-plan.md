# The Boulos Plan

## Overview

The Boulos plan is the US mediation track named after Massad Boulos, President Trump's senior adviser for Arab and African affairs. It proposes a negotiated merger of the two executives rather than an election-first transition: Abdulhamid Dbeibah would remain prime minister, while Saddam Haftar, son of the eastern commander, would become head of the Presidential Council. The arrangement has been discussed with both camps but has not been signed or formally published.

## The Proposal

The plan distributes the two most powerful offices between the families that currently control the west and east. It preserves the Tripoli government's administrative apparatus and gives the Haftar family a national presidency, with military unification to follow. Its appeal to Washington is speed: a single executive can be presented as reunification without the risk of an election that might produce an unacceptable winner or no result at all.

## Why Turkey Holds a Veto

Turkey's military presence in western Libya and its new maritime memorandum with the eastern parliament give Ankara leverage over both sides. Turkish officials have told Saddam Haftar that Ankara's support depends on the deal, and the eastern parliament ratified the 2019 maritime memorandum five days after the speaker blocking it was removed. Turkey has committed to nothing in return, and any unified government would inherit the maritime agreement.

## The Critique

Libyan and European analysts describe the plan as an elite bargain that would entrench the two families and postpone accountability. It would also formalise the armed groups' role rather than dissolve them. The High Council of State and several parties object that it bypasses the 4+4 process and the constitutional framework, while the US has warned that obstructors could face sanctions.

## Current Status

The plan remains the most concrete route to a single executive, but it is not the only track. The UN process continues, the House has proposed its own joint committee, and the central bank crisis is setting a financial clock that may force a decision before any of these formats produces one.
