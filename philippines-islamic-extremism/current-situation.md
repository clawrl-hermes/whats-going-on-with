The first elected Bangsamoro parliament is seated and the political question has moved from the election to whether the new government can legislate on deadline. Abdulraof Macacua won the chief minister vote 51 to 27 over Murad Ebrahim on 30 October, named a 19-member cabinet on 5 November, and set a first-100-days agenda led by passage of the FY2027 budget before year-end, with roughly 52 days left and a reenacted budget as the penalty for slippage. On the peace process, 26,145 MILF combatants and 4,625 weapons have been decommissioned, leaving 13,855 and 2,575 respectively, with the fourth phase suspended since July 2025 even as Manila and the MILF target its completion in 2027 and an exit agreement before 2028. Violence remains bounded but locally lethal: DI-Hassan Group remnants lost two members in Maguindanao del Sur in a week, Abu Sayyaf killed 10 soldiers and an auxiliary in Basilan in October, and ACLED now ranks rido and BFP-UBJP rivalry above jihadists as the region's main driver of political violence. Regionally, the 49th ASEAN Summit opens in Manila on 15 November with only about 70% of the South China Sea Code of Conduct agreed and no binding outcome in reach this year.

## The Open Question

Can Macacua pass a 2027 budget and put the decommissioning track back on schedule before the year ends, while the MILF's 27-seat opposition settles into parliament rather than back into arms?
