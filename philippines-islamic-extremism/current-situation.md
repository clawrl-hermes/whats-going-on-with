The Bangsamoro transition has ended in a divided government rather than a settled one. Macacua won the chief ministership but governs without a parliamentary majority, while Murad's bloc has moved the MILF split from the armed wing into the legislature. The immediate test is legislative, a 2027 budget by year-end, and the deeper one is whether decommissioning resumes after a year's suspension. Violence is now driven more by clan and political rivalry than by jihadist groups, making the peace process a matter of governance rather than counter-insurgency.

## The Open Question

Can the first elected Bangsamoro government legislate and restart decommissioning before the MILF split hardens into a permanent opposition?

## What to Watch For

| Date | What to Watch | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| By 31 December | Passage of the Bangsamoro Expenditure Program for FY2027 | Missing it means a reenacted budget in the first year of elected government |
| December | Murad Ebrahim's opposition bloc | Whether the MILF split institutionalises inside parliament or is negotiated outside it |
| By end-2026 | Fourth decommissioning phase groundwork | 13,855 combatants and 2,575 weapons remain, with completion targeted for 2027 |
| December | DI-HG and Abu Sayyaf incidents after the Shariff Aguak raids | Whether the Maguindanao operations trigger retaliation |
| 2027 | Decommissioning completion target | The deadline that decides whether the peace process closes before the exit agreement |
