# Priority Sources — Islamic Extremism in the Philippines

These sources should be checked first during every source monitor run, before conducting broader Tavily searches. They are organized by category reflecting source value and trust level.

Compiled 2026-11-11 from the topic's existing source base and the high-signal source types in `domain-notes.md`. Sites marked "bot-blocked" often still work through search; if extraction fails, search the outlet by name.

---

## Regional Specialists (high trust)

These outlets provide dedicated coverage of the Philippines and Mindanao. Check each for new articles.

- **MindaNews** — https://mindanews.com
  Mindanao-based independent newsroom. The deepest coverage of BARMM politics, the peace process and conflict; often ahead of the Manila outlets.
- **Rappler** — https://www.rappler.com
  Manila digital outlet with strong Mindanao reporting and investigative work. Critical of the administration, so separate its reporting from its framing.
- **Inquirer** — https://www.inquirer.net
  The Philippines' largest broadsheet. Fast on incidents, official statements and congressional reaction.
- **Philippine News Agency (PNA)** — https://www.pna.gov.ph
  State-run news agency. Carries official statements and government framing; useful for what Manila wants said, not for independent verification.
- **SunStar** — https://www.sunstar.com.ph
  Local newspaper chain with strong Cebu and Mindanao coverage. Fast on incidents and local politics.
- **BenarNews** — https://www.benarnews.org/english/
  Regional outlet covering the Philippines, Indonesia and Malaysia. Good on militancy, security operations and maritime disputes.
- **GMA News** — https://www.gmanetwork.com/news
  Broadcast and digital news. Fast incident coverage and official interviews.
- **Philippine Center for Investigative Journalism (PCIJ)** — https://pcij.org
  Independent investigative journalism on governance, elections and impunity.

## Think Tanks & Research Institutions (analytical depth)

These sources provide longer-form analysis, policy recommendations, and original research. Useful for context and deepening understanding, not for breaking news.

- **International Crisis Group** — https://www.crisisgroup.org/asia/south-east-asia/philippines
  The best structural analysis of the peace process, policing and conflict risk. Bot-blocked; use search if extraction fails.
- **Institute for Policy Analysis of Conflict (IPAC)** — https://understandingconflict.org
  Jakarta-based conflict analysis. Reports on Philippine militant groups and regional jihadist networks.
- **RSIS** — https://www.rsis.edu.sg
  Singapore defence and security school. Threat assessments and the "diffusing violence" thesis. Bot-blocked; use search.
- **FULCRUM** — https://fulcrum.sg
  RSIS commentary platform. Short analysis on Southeast Asian security and Philippine politics.
- **ACLED** — https://acleddata.com
  Event data and periodic analysis. The best read on which groups are actually active; corrects outlet claims about "resurgent" groups.
- **United States Institute of Peace (USIP)** — https://www.usip.org
  Analysis of mediation, decommissioning and peace-process design.

## Official Sources (primary — biased, monitor for policy statements)

Direct statements from governments and agencies. Essential for tracking agreements, security operations and election administration. Treat as primary sources — the framing is always self-serving.

- **Office of the Presidential Adviser on Peace, Reconciliation and Unity (OPAPRU)** — https://peace.gov.ph
  Government peace panel. Agreements, decommissioning and normalisation milestones. Bot-blocked; use search.
- **Bangsamoro Government** — https://bangsamoro.gov.ph
  Cabinet, parliament and budget announcements from the autonomous government.
- **Commission on Elections (Comelec)** — https://comelec.gov.ph
  Election administration, disqualifications, results and security arrangements. Bot-blocked; use search.
- **Philippine National Police (PNP)** — https://pnp.gov.ph
  Police statements on incidents, arrests and security operations. Bot-blocked; use search.
- **ASEAN** — https://asean.org
  Summit statements, the Code of Conduct process and maritime diplomacy.

## Advocacy & Civil Society (useful signal — biased framing)

These sources represent specific constituencies. Useful for tracking rights allegations, displacement and impunity. Always cross-reference with regional specialists.

- **Karapatan** — https://karapatan.org
  Human rights coalition. Documentation of abuses, political persecution and counter-insurgency impact.
- **Amnesty International Philippines** — https://www.amnesty.org.ph
  Rights research and campaigns. Reports on impunity and security-force conduct.
- **Human Rights Watch — Philippines** — https://www.hrw.org/asia/philippines
  International rights reporting. Strong on extrajudicial killings and policy analysis.
- **ICRC Philippines** — https://www.icrc.org/en/where-we-work/philippines
  Humanitarian law, displacement and detention in conflict-affected communities.

---

## Source Selection Guidance

1. **Check regional specialists first** — visit/extract each for new articles since the last run
2. **Then check official sources** — scan for new agreements, operations, appointments or election decisions
3. **Then check think tanks** — for analytical context on recent developments
4. **Then check advocacy sources** — for rights allegations and displacement reporting
5. **Finally, run Tavily searches** — to catch anything the curated sources missed
