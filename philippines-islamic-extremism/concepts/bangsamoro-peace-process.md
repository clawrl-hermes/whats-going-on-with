# Bangsamoro Peace Process

## Overview

The Bangsamoro peace process implements the 2014 Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro between the MILF and the Philippine government. It replaced a separatist war with an autonomous region, BARMM, created in 2019, and a transition government led by the former rebels. The agreement has two tracks: a political track that ends with an elected parliament, and a normalization track that disarms, reintegrates and polices the region. The first is now complete. The second is not.

## Structure

The political track was designed to convert the MILF from an armed movement into a party competing for votes. It created an 80-seat parliament with party, district and sectoral seats, a chief minister chosen by parliament, and a transition period that was extended three times before the September 2026 election. The normalization track runs in parallel: phased decommissioning of BIAF combatants and weapons, socioeconomic packages for former fighters, transitional justice for conflict victims, and the gradual transfer of policing from the military to a civilian force.

## The 2026 Election

The first parliamentary election, held on 14 September 2026, produced 84.34% turnout and a hung parliament: the BFP won 31 seats, the UBJP 30 and the Bangsamoro Grand Coalition 15. The result was a credible vote and a fragmented one. It ended the interim government and gave the MILF's two factions a shared institutional stake, but it also moved the contest inside the political class rather than resolving it.

## The Normalization Gap

Normalization is behind schedule. Only about 65% of combatants and 64% of weapons had been processed by mid-2026, the 30 October transition deadline could not be met, and decommissioning had been suspended over the government's failure to deliver socioeconomic packages. The exit agreement, now promised before 2028, is meant to close the gap; the fourth and final decommissioning phase is targeted for 2027. Until then, weapons remain in the hands of units whose political loyalties are contested.

## Current Status

The political track is finished and the normalization track is the binding constraint. A fragmented MILF, an underfunded reintegration programme and clan violence in Maguindanao mean the process can still fail after its democratic milestone. The next tests are the 2027 budget, the resumption of decommissioning, and whether the new government can deliver services that make peace pay.
