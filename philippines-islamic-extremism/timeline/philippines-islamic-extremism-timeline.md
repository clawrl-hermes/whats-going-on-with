# Timeline: Islamic Extremism in the Philippines

## 1991
- Abu Sayyaf splits from the Moro National Liberation Front under Abdurajak Abubakar Janjalani, seeking a radical Islamist state in Mindanao.

## 2004
- Abu Sayyaf bombs the MV Superferry 14 in Manila Bay in February, killing 116 in the Philippines' deadliest terrorist attack.

## 2014
- The government and the MILF sign the Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro in March.
- Abu Sayyaf leader Isnilon Hapilon pledges allegiance to ISIS.

## 2017
- IS-affiliated Maute and Abu Sayyaf fighters seize Marawi in May; the military retakes the city in October after five months, with more than 1,200 killed and 350,000 displaced.

## 2019
- The Bangsamoro Organic Law is ratified and BARMM replaces ARMM.
- Duterte orders an "all-out war" on Abu Sayyaf after the Jolo cathedral bombing, significantly degrading the group.

## 2020
- The Anti-Terrorism Act expands surveillance and detention powers.

## 2024
- The Supreme Court rules that Sulu is not part of BARMM, removing seven parliamentary seats and reshaping the 2026 electoral map.

## 2025
- **March:**
  - Marcos appoints Abdulraof Macacua interim chief minister without consulting the MILF Central Committee, setting up the split with Murad Ebrahim.

## 2026
- **February:**
  - Suspected Abu Sayyaf remnants kill four soldiers in Patikul, Sulu, signalling regrouping.
- **March:**
  - Marcos signs Republic Act 12317, resetting the parliamentary election to 14 September.
- **May:**
  - The Bangsamoro Federalist Party splits from the UBJP; Mohagher Iqbal is removed as education minister.
  - The MILF and the Philippine Army declare a joint front against ISIS affiliates in Lanao del Norte.
- **June:**
  - The MILF Central Committee suspends Macacua as BIAF chief of staff.
  - A magnitude-7.8 earthquake off Sarangani kills more than 65 people and affects over 700,000.
- **July:**
  - Marcos appoints Mel Sarmiento to chair the government peace panel.
  - The election gun ban takes effect on 16 July; a town councilor is shot dead the same day.
  - The campaign opens with 631 candidates for 80 seats.
  - Marcos's SONA gives the peace process 40 seconds and no mention of the peace agreement.
- **August:**
  - Macacua survives an ambush hours after being named BFP chair.
  - DI-Hassan and DI-Maute leaders are killed in separate operations.
  - The government and MILF sign a Declaration of Continuity on the peace agreement.
- **September:**
  - The MILF Central Committee formally removes Macacua and 40 other BIAF officers.
  - The election is held on 14 September: turnout reaches 84.34% and no party wins a majority.
  - The BFP wins 31 seats, the UBJP 30 and the BGC 15 in the first elected parliament.
  - The GPH and MILF panels meet in Kuala Lumpur and sign six agreements on normalization.
- **October:**
  - The BFP-led coalition secures a 46-seat majority for Macacua.
  - On 30 October the first elected parliament is sworn in and elects Macacua chief minister with 51 votes against 27 for Murad Ebrahim.
- **November:**
  - Macacua names a 19-member cabinet on 5 November.
  - Marcos meets Murad in Malacañang and both sides commit to an exit agreement before 2028 and the final decommissioning phase by 2027.
