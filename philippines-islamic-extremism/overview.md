This topic tracks the trajectory of Islamist militancy in the southern Philippines and the peace process built to contain it. It covers Abu Sayyaf, Dawlah Islamiyah, the BIFF and the splinter groups that emerged from the Moro insurgencies, alongside the Bangsamoro transition and the politics of the autonomous region.

The central argument is that the threat has changed rather than disappeared. Large-scale jihadist violence peaked with the 2017 Marawi siege and has declined since, but it has been replaced by a more diffuse landscape of clan feuds, private armed groups, election violence, youth radicalisation and disaffected former combatants.

Coverage follows the Bangsamoro peace process and its normalization track, the MILF's internal split, the 2026 parliamentary election, security-force reform, and the way South China Sea tensions compete with Mindanao for national attention.
