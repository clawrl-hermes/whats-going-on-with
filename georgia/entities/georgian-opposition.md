# Georgian Opposition

Georgia's opposition is a fragmented set of parties that agree on rejecting Georgian Dream and little else. It ranges from the UNM and Coalition for Change to Lelo, For Georgia and the Federalists, with different histories and no shared leader. Five of them, which won more than 800,000 votes in 2024, are now the target of a constitutional ban.

## Key Actions

- **October 2024** — Contests disputed parliamentary elections; most parties boycott the new parliament.
- **October 2025** — For Georgia ends its boycott, becoming the only opposition party in parliament.
- **2025–2026** — Street protests against the government pass 500 days.
- **July 2026** — Eight opposition leaders face sabotage charges carrying up to 15 years.
- **September 2026** — UNM leader Baramidze is detained; Georgian Dream adds For Georgia to the ban lawsuit.
- **October 2026** — Constitutional Court hearings open on banning five parties.

## Current Position

The opposition is being removed from electoral politics before the 2027 elections. Five parties are in the ban case, its most prominent leaders are detained or in exile, and only For Georgia still holds seats. For Georgia's 12 seats are the only parliamentary bridge, and its leader Gakharia has been in exile since 2025. The rest operate through media, street protests and foreign capitals, which the government can portray as external interference. It has public sympathy but no unified leadership or programme, which lets the government proceed party by party.

## Constraints

- The Constitutional Court case and criminal prosecutions remove its leaders and legal standing.
- Fragmentation leaves it without a common platform or candidate.
- Exile leadership and public fatigue weaken its ability to mobilise.
