# United States

The United States is the only actor that could change Georgia's security calculus, but it has chosen a transactional reset over restoring the partnership. Its leverage is real but narrow: sanctions, visa policy, military cooperation and the promise of high-level attention.

## Key Actions

- **November 2024** — Suspends the strategic partnership after the foreign agents law.
- **March 2026** — Rubio calls Kobakhidze, the first high-level contact since the suspension.
- **June 2026** — The House passes HR 7668 on Chinese and Russian influence in the Caucasus.
- **August 2026** — Trump sends a personal letter; the visa bond becomes permanent; the White House names Georgia in China's tariff-circumvention network.
- **September 2026** — Kobakhidze meets Trump and Rubio at the UN General Assembly.
- **2026** — The ambassador's post stays vacant; policy centres on Armenia and TRIPP.

## Current Position

Washington keeps a channel open and is exploring a reset, but it has not restored the partnership or filled the ambassador's post. Congress is moving toward conditionality while the administration prioritises Armenia, the Middle Corridor and a Trump Tower project. The White House's priority is avoiding a new front rather than winning Georgia back, and the tower gives it a business channel that bypasses the suspended partnership. Congress's conditionality bills and the administration's transactional diplomacy pull in different directions, and Tbilisi exploits the gap.

## Constraints

- Georgia is peripheral to Ukraine and China policy.
- Congress and the executive disagree on how much to demand.
- Russia's escalation dominance limits what Washington can offer without security guarantees.
