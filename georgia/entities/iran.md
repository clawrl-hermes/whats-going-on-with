# Iran

Iran is Georgia's newest major influence channel and the clearest test of Georgian Dream's multi-vector doctrine. Its footprint runs through trade, transport and a network of shell companies, and it has become a friction point in US-Georgia relations.

## Key Actions

- **2025** — A deputy foreign minister signals solidarity with Tehran.
- **March 2026** — The Hudson Institute documents nearly 13,000 Iranian-linked companies in Georgia.
- **August 2026** — Georgia refuses to join the EU's updated sanctions on Iran.
- **September 2026** — Ambassador Moujani's remarks on Queen Ketevan prompt a summons; Georgia complies with US sanctions on Iranian airlines.
- **August 2026** — OFAC sanctions a Georgia-registered crypto firm for facilitating Iranian payments.
- **October 2026** — Kobakhidze meets President Pezeshkian in Yerevan.

## Current Position

Iran uses Georgia as a sanctions-evasion and transit route and has built a political channel to the government. Tbilisi protects the relationship from Western pressure but concedes on visible US demands, leaving Tehran with influence without a formal alliance. The relationship is deliberately low-visibility: no treaties, few official visits, and a government that denies strategic alignment while blocking Western pressure. The Hudson Institute report gave US lawmakers a ready case against Tbilisi, and Georgia's compliance with the airline sanctions shows it still calibrates to Washington.

## Constraints

- US sanctions enforcement and OFAC designations limit the relationship's financial reach.
- Georgia still depends on Western finance and the Middle Corridor.
- Public and church sensitivity over Iranian historical claims constrains Tehran's cultural outreach.
