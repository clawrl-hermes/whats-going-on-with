# Russia

Russia occupies about 20% of Georgia's territory and is the actor Georgian Dream most fears. Its objective is not to block Georgian connectivity but to keep it dependent on Russian routes, markets and goodwill. It also controls the terms of the two frozen conflicts and can escalate without crossing the threshold of open war.

## Key Actions

- **2008** — Defeats Georgia in a five-day war and recognises Abkhazia and South Ossetia.
- **2022** — Invades Ukraine; Georgia declines to join Western sanctions.
- **2025** — Reclassifies Georgia as "relatively friendly" and holds the first Putin-Kobakhidze encounter in 15 years.
- **June–July 2026** — Gagloev resigns and Kambolov becomes acting leader in South Ossetia; a leaked report reveals a ₽13 billion Russian soft-power programme.
- **September 2026** — Kambolov wins the South Ossetian election with 85%; Medvedev calls the 2008 recognition "geopolitical justice".
- **October 2026** — Bans imports from seven Georgian companies and demands non-use-of-force guarantees.

## Current Position

Moscow has kept Georgia out of NATO and the EU without annexing it, using trade, energy, occupied territory and soft power. It rewards accommodation with dialogue but not concessions, and the October import ban shows it will use economic coercion regardless of Georgian restraint. The preferred outcome is a Georgia that is formally sovereign, economically dependent and permanently outside Western institutions.

## Constraints

- The war in Ukraine consumes military and financial capacity.
- Georgia's transit routes give Moscow leverage but require a stable Tbilisi.
- Borderization and sanctions push Georgian public opinion toward the EU.
