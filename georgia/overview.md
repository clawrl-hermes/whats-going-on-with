This topic tracks Georgia's position between Russia, the West, China and Iran, and the domestic politics that drive it. Georgian Dream has governed since 2012 on a doctrine of regime survival: avoid provoking Moscow, keep enough Western engagement to preserve leverage, and deepen ties with Beijing and Tehran where they cost little.

The through-line is strategic drift. Georgia's transit value — the Middle Corridor, the Baku-Tbilisi-Kars railway, the Black Sea ports — is real, but it is eroding as Armenia, Azerbaijan and Central Asia gain alternatives. At the same time, the government has narrowed civic space, moved to ban the opposition and frozen its EU accession path.

Coverage follows the corridors and the competition over them, the US and EU response, Russia's occupation and economic leverage, the South Ossetia absorption, and the domestic slide toward one-party rule.
