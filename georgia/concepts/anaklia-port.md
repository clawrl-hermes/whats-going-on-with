# Anaklia Port

## Overview

Anaklia is Georgia's flagship deep-water port, planned on the Black Sea coast to handle the largest vessels and anchor the Middle Corridor. It has been the country's most contested infrastructure project for a decade: launched in 2016, cancelled in 2020, re-awarded to a Chinese consortium in 2024, and abandoned by that consortium in July 2026. The stakes are not only cargo throughput; the port determines whether Georgia can offer a credible alternative to Russian-controlled routes.

## Structure

The project is now organised under a "landlord" model. The state retains ownership of the core infrastructure and invites international partners into limited operational roles, rather than conceding a single strategic stake. Dredging and breakwater work are contracted to Jan De Nul, which is preparing a 17.5-metre channel so the port can take the largest Black Sea vessels. The first phase is targeted for 2029. The model lowers dependence on any one power, but it also leaves the project without a strategic anchor or a committed source of cargo.

## Strategic Significance

Anaklia is the maritime end of the Middle Corridor and the only Georgian port designed for deep-draft vessels. A working port would let Tbilisi sell itself as the Black Sea gateway for Central Asian and Chinese cargo moving west without Russia, and would give Western governments a commercial reason to stay engaged. That is why the Chinese stake drew congressional attention in HR 7668, and why the state's decision to invite China, Central Asia and Azerbaijan but not Europe raised questions about the project's direction.

## Current Status

China's exit removed the project's main investor and its flagship link to the June 2026 strategic partnership. The government is now courting China, Central Asia and Azerbaijan for limited roles, while European partners have not been invited. Physical work continues: a second dredger arrived in September and about a kilometre of breakwater base is complete. The 2029 first-phase target holds on paper, but the project still lacks a strategic investor, a cargo base and a financing structure.
