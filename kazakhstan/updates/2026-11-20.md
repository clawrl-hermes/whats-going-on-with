- **Corruption arrests decapitate the state holding company** — The KNB's anti-corruption service detained Samruk-Kazyna chief executive Nurlan Zhakupov and Kazakhstan Temir Zholy head Yelzhas Otynshiyev on 11 November, along with several unnamed officials, over a corruption-related offence. The fund manages roughly $88 billion in oil, uranium, rail and telecom assets; Tokayev called the case "an extremely serious signal of large-scale corruption in state-owned companies," and Prime Minister Olzhas Bektenov told investors on 18 November that a new chief executive would be named and board oversight tightened ([Times of Central Asia](https://timesca.com/what-is-samruk-kazyna), [Times of Central Asia](https://timesca.com/kazakhstan-knb-chief-replaced-corruption-arrests)).
- **The purge reaches the security service** — Tokayev replaced National Security Committee chairman Yermek Sagimbayev with Marat Irmenov on 16 November, five days after the detentions and with no reason given, then told the new KNB leadership to reform the service and protect the country's economic interests. Removing the spy chief in the middle of an anti-corruption case widens the shake-up beyond the companies themselves ([Times of Central Asia](https://timesca.com/kazakhstan-knb-chief-replaced-corruption-arrests)).
- **UK sanctions on SIBUR put two flagship polymer plants at risk** — Britain designated the Russian petrochemical group on 12 November, catching its 40% stake in the KPI polypropylene plant and 30% stake in the $7.4 billion Silleno polyethylene complex, which was 40.9% complete on 30 October. The joint ventures are not automatically frozen, but Kazakh bankers and suppliers now have to decide whether the compliance burden is worth the business ([Kursiv](https://kz.kursiv.media/en/2026-11-13/engk-tank-uk-imposes-sanctions-on-russias-sibur-kazakhstan-could-feel-the-impact), [Times of Central Asia](https://timesca.com/sibur-sanctions-test-kazakhstans-petrochemical-ambitions)).
- **The IMF tells the National Bank to stop cutting before 4 December** — After consultations from 4 to 17 November, the IMF mission said "a higher policy rate appears warranted," citing services inflation, elevated expectations and budget and National Fund spending. Annual inflation was 9.2% in October against a 5% target; the IMF sees 9.6% at end-2026 and a return to target only in 2029, while the National Bank expects 2028 ([Times of Central Asia](https://timesca.com/imf-kazakhstan-base-rate-increase)).
- **The Caspian Bridge gets commercial contracts** — Samruk-Kazyna and Azerbaijan's AZCON signed a cooperation agreement in early November to prepare and implement the Caspian Bridge system, and Kazakhstan Temir Zholy and Azerbaijan Railways signed a term sheet for a joint intermodal terminal at Alat. The two states and Georgia are working to a 30 million tonne Middle Corridor target for 2029 ([EUalive](https://eualive.net/kazakhstan-and-azerbaijan-advance-the-caspian-bridge-as-core-upgrade-to-the-middle-corridor)).
- **Growth holds at 4.5% as manufacturing outpaces industry** — GDP rose 4.5% in January-October, 0.3 percentage points above the first nine months, with the non-oil sector up 5.6%, industrial output up 3.5% and manufacturing up 9.3%. Rail freight with China reached 31.8 million tons over the same period, an 8% increase ([Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2026/11/kazakhstans-economic-growth-up-4-5-in-first-10-months-of-2026), [Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2026/11/kazakhstan-china-freight-traffic-rises-8-in-first-10-months-of-2026)).
