# Timeline: Kazakhstan — Global Competitiveness

## 1991
- Kazakhstan declares independence from the Soviet Union in December; Nursultan Nazarbayev becomes president.

## 1993
- The oil and gas sector opens to foreign investment, drawing Chevron, ExxonMobil and Shell into Tengiz, Kashagan and Karachaganak.

## 2003
- China and Kazakhstan sign early pipeline and infrastructure agreements, precursors to the Belt and Road Initiative.

## 2008
- The global financial crisis hits the commodity-dependent economy; the government launches an anti-crisis programme.

## 2009
- Samruk-Kazyna is created to consolidate state assets and stabilise the banking sector.

## 2010
- Kazakhstan chairs the OSCE, signalling its ambition to be recognised as a serious international actor.

## 2013
- China announces the Belt and Road Initiative; Kazakhstan becomes a key transit corridor for China-Europe trade.

## 2014
- Oil prices collapse and the economy enters recession.

## 2015
- The tenge is devalued by about 40% and the government launches the "100 Concrete Steps" reform programme.

## 2017
- Astana hosts EXPO 2017 on "Future Energy" and the government begins targeting renewable energy and fintech.

## 2019
- Kassym-Jomart Tokayev succeeds Nazarbayev in June, retaining Nazarbayev as Security Council chairman and "Leader of the Nation".

## 2020
- The pandemic disrupts the economy and accelerates the digitalisation of government services.

## 2022
- Fuel-price protests turn violent in Almaty in January; Tokayev orders a crackdown and requests CSTO intervention.
- Russia's invasion of Ukraine reshapes Kazakhstan's strategic calculus and begins its sanctions-balancing act.
- Cryptocurrency mining surges as Chinese miners relocate, straining the power grid.

## 2023
- Tokayev consolidates power and removes Nazarbayev from key positions; an AI and digital transformation strategy is announced.

## 2024
- Cryptocurrency mining is partially banned after grid instability; the EU-Central Asia summit in Samarkand signals growing European interest.

## 2025
- Kazakhstan enters the top 10 of Dealroom's "Rising Stars" tech index and Higgsfield AI becomes its first unicorn.
- Russia-Kazakhstan trade reaches a record $29 billion despite the war.
- China-Kazakhstan trade grows past $48 billion.

## 2026
- **February:**
  - The government unveils a $400 billion investment target for 2025-2029 and reports non-oil growth of 5.4%.
- **March:**
  - A new constitution is approved by referendum, creating a unicameral Kurultai and a vice-presidency.
- **April:**
  - The EU adopts its 20th sanctions package with a new third-country circumvention tool.
  - Kazakhstan climbs 13 places to 42nd in the World Bank's Human Capital Index Plus.
- **May:**
  - Putin visits Astana; 15 documents are signed and trade is reported at a record $29 billion.
  - The China-Kazakhstan grain trading platform opens, and Turkey's TPAO enters the energy market.
- **June:**
  - The $10 billion NVIDIA and Firebird Data Center Valley project is signed for Ekibastuz.
  - Tokayev visits Brussels, securing $12 billion in deals including 50 Airbus jets.
  - China and Kazakhstan sign a civil nuclear protocol; CNNC is selected for two reactors.
  - Kazakhstan joins the US-led AI Opportunity Partnership.
  - A US-backed consortium secures the Northern Katpar tungsten deposit.
- **July:**
  - Drone strikes on tankers halt CPC loadings, cutting about 80% of Kazakh oil exports.
  - Tokayev tells Putin in Omsk that the Ukraine war should be frozen.
  - Tokayev visits China; $15 billion in agreements are signed and WAICO is launched.
  - A 15-hectare terminal at Iran's Bandar Abbas is announced.
  - The petroleum export ban is extended to May 2027.
- **August:**
  - Manufacturing output exceeds mining for the first time.
  - A White House report names Kazakhstan a Tier III conduit for Chinese tariff evasion.
  - The first Kurultai election gives the pro-presidential Adilet party 71% on 74% turnout.
  - The US presses Kazakhstan to choose between Pax Silica and WAICO.
- **September:**
  - Tokayev's Seoul visit produces about 80 agreements worth $19 billion.
  - Middle Corridor freight rises 22% and East-West transit 55%.
  - The Kazakhstan-China Investment Forum signs 50 documents worth $8.2 billion.
  - Tokayev's Berlin visit yields about 30 agreements worth $2 billion.
- **October:**
  - Kazakhstan's startup ecosystem is valued at 1,500 companies and forecast venture funding above $460 million.
  - Germany and Kazakhstan deepen economic ties despite differences over Ukraine.
