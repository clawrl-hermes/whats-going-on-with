Kazakhstan is trying to keep three balances at once: between Washington and Beijing on AI, between Russia and the West on oil routes, and between fiscal ambition and a narrow revenue base. Ukrainian drone attacks have cost roughly 3.5 million tonnes of CPC output, pushing Astana toward BTC, Baku-Supsa and eastbound capacity, while Tokayev's 28 October address promised a $14 billion "People's Construction" programme and an electricity surplus by 2029. On AI, Kazakhstan has joined both Pax Silica and WAICO and secured a US licence for 45,000 NVIDIA chips, a hedge that depends on neither side forcing a choice. The uranium law's processing requirement has meanwhile tilted the minerals file toward China's integrated model.

## The Open Question

Can Tokayev keep the AI and minerals hedge between Washington and Beijing while CPC disruption forces a permanent reroute of oil exports?
