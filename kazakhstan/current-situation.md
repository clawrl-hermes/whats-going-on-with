Kazakhstan's economy is diversifying faster than its institutions can absorb. Manufacturing now exceeds mining, non-oil growth is above 5%, and investment is shifting from extraction into processing, but the detention of the Samruk-Kazyna and rail chiefs shows how much of that programme depends on a handful of state managers. The external hedge remains intact — China, the EU, the US and Russia all stay engaged — while the CPC's exposure to the Ukraine war keeps the oil rent vulnerable and sanctions pressure raises the compliance cost of every new project. The binding constraint is no longer demand or capital; it is execution and the credibility of the state companies that deliver it.

## The Open Question

Can Kazakhstan convert its oil rent into industrial capacity faster than the state institutions managing it lose credibility?
