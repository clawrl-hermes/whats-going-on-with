# Tech Pivot

## Overview

Kazakhstan's tech pivot is an attempt to convert energy and mineral wealth into a knowledge economy before the hydrocarbon rent declines. The instruments are a Ministry of AI and Digital Development, the Astana Hub tech park, a national AI law, and a venture fund-of-funds. The goal is not to compete with the United States or China but to become the middle power that hosts their compute, trains their engineers and sells digital services across Eurasia.

## The AI Bet

The anchor project is Data Center Valley at Ekibastuz, a $10 billion package with Firebird and NVIDIA that is planned to host up to 100,000 GPUs. In June 2026 the government signed the deal, and in October Firebird secured a US export licence for 45,000 Grace Blackwell chips, the first licence of its kind. NVIDIA's Jensen Huang visited Astana and framed the country as able to "transform energy into intelligence". Kazakhstan joined the US-led AI Opportunity Partnership in June and became a founding member of China's World AI Cooperation Organisation in July.

## The Startup Economy

The ecosystem is small but growing quickly. Venture funding reached $209 million in 2025, the country has about 1,500 startups, and Higgsfield AI became its first unicorn. IT services exports reached $1.1 billion. The government's Digital Qazaqstan strategy targets three global unicorns by 2029 and basic AI skills for 80% of graduates. Most capital still comes from state-linked sources, and the two-hub split between Almaty and Astana weakens the network effect.

## The Squeeze

The tech pivot has run into the US-China rivalry. Washington told countries seeking Pax Silica cooperation that they could not join WAICO; Kazakhstan had already signed both, and refused to renounce either. The Kazakh and Uzbek academies responded by announcing a joint AI research platform. Analysts argued that an ultimatum would push Astana toward Beijing, because China's infrastructure ties are deeper and its market is closer.

## Current Status

The pivot is real but not yet an engine. Compute capacity, venture funding and exports are all rising from a low base, and the economy still depends on oil and metals. The binding constraint is political and external: the country can attract both AI blocs, but it cannot keep both if Washington makes access conditional. Kazakhstan is betting that hosting infrastructure is neutral. The bet is untested.
