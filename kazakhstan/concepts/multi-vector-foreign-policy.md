# Multi-Vector Foreign Policy

## Overview

Multi-vectorism is Kazakhstan's founding foreign-policy doctrine: maintain productive relations with Russia, China, the United States, the EU and regional partners without aligning with any bloc. The logic is geographic. A landlocked state between two nuclear powers cannot change its neighbours, so it tries to make itself useful to all of them. Under Tokayev the doctrine has shifted from defensive balancing to what analysts call "sovereign connectivity statecraft" — converting diplomatic openness into routes, platforms and services that others depend on.

## How It Works

Five instruments recur. Russia supplies security guarantees, the CPC export route and northern transit. China supplies investment, the eastern pipeline and the Belt and Road. The West supplies technology, capital and market access. Regional bodies — the Organization of Turkic States, the SCO, C5+1 — provide collective cover. And Kazakhstan offers its territory as a connector: the Middle Corridor, the Trans-Caspian route, data cables and AI compute. The method is to sign with everyone and let each partner believe it has a stake in Astana's stability.

## The Deal Pipeline

The strategy is measured in agreements. In 2025-26 Tokayev concluded roughly $17 billion of deals in Washington, $12 billion in Brussels, $15 billion in China, $19 billion in South Korea, $2 billion in Germany and a record $29 billion of trade with Russia. The aggregate pipeline exceeds $60 billion. Each package is presented as diversification, and each creates a new dependency: Chinese loans, European technology, Korean processing, Russian energy.

## Where It Is Tested

Three pressures are narrowing the space. Drone strikes on the CPC in July cut about 80% of oil exports and showed that Russia's war can reach Kazakhstan directly. Washington's demand that Pax Silica members stay out of China's WAICO turned AI into a loyalty test. And Western sanctions enforcement — the EU's 21st package, the US Sanctioning Russia Act, the Treasury's tariff-evasion report — raises the cost of being a transit state. Kazakhstan has responded by refusing to export refined fuel to Russia, lobbying Kyiv to spare CPC infrastructure and keeping both AI memberships.

## Current Status

Multi-vectorism is still working, but it is becoming more expensive. The deals keep coming and no partner has forced a formal choice. The risk is cumulative: each crisis requires a concession, and the concessions are eroding the neutrality that made the strategy valuable. The open question is whether Kazakhstan can keep building options after the options start to conflict.
