# Ukraine

Ukraine matters to Kazakhstan because its drones reach the pipeline that carries most Kazakh oil. The CPC runs through Russian territory to Novorossiysk, and Ukraine targets the terminal and tankers to cut Moscow's export revenue. Kazakhstan is not the intended target, but it absorbs the cost: loadings stop, Tengiz output falls and budget revenue disappears.

## Key Actions

- **July 2026:** Drone strikes on four tankers near the CPC terminal halted loadings and cut about 80% of Kazakh oil exports.
- **July 2026:** Tokayev used the Omsk forum to tell Putin the war should be frozen; Kyiv welcomed the message.
- **August 2026:** After Kazakh lobbying in Washington, US officials pressed Ukraine to pledge not to strike infrastructure handling Kazakh oil.
- **October 2026:** Strikes on Russian energy infrastructure resumed, and Kazakhstan reaffirmed its Russian oil supply agreement through 2028.

## Current Position

Ukraine treats the CPC as a legitimate military target because the same system carries Russian crude and funds the war. It has tried to avoid alienating Astana, welcoming Tokayev's calls for a freeze and accepting Western pressure to spare Kazakh cargo. But the distinction between Kazakh and Russian barrels is hard to maintain in a campaign against a shared terminal.

## Constraints

- Ukraine depends on US and European support, which gives Washington leverage over its targeting.
- It cannot easily separate Kazakh from Russian oil in the CPC system.
- Strikes that hurt Kazakhstan risk pushing Astana closer to Moscow, the opposite of Kyiv's interest.
