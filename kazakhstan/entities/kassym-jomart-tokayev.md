# Kassym-Jomart Tokayev

Tokayev has run Kazakhstan since 2019 and has made the country's foreign policy personal. His project is to preserve presidential control while opening enough channels to Russia, China, the West and the Gulf that no single partner can dictate terms. The 2026 constitutional changes and the Kurultai election completed the transfer of power away from the Nazarbayev-era system into his own.

## Key Actions

- **January 2022:** Ordered the crackdown on fuel-price protests and requested CSTO intervention, then removed Nazarbayev from remaining posts.
- **March 2026:** A referendum approved a new constitution creating a unicameral Kurultai, a vice-presidency and a strengthened presidency.
- **July 2026:** Told Putin at the Omsk forum that the Ukraine war should be frozen, proposing an "Istanbul 2.0" format.
- **August 2026:** The first Kurultai election gave the pro-presidential Adilet party 71% on 74% turnout; he signed amendments removing Nazarbayev from the Foreign Policy Concept.
- **September 2026:** At the SCO summit in Bishkek he defined the doctrine as "connecting, not choosing", and proposed an International Water Organisation.
- **October 2026:** In his State of the Nation address he proposed a strategic uranium reserve and a $14 billion "People's Construction" programme.

## Current Position

Tokayev has consolidated more power than any Kazakh leader since 2019 and has used it to keep every external partner engaged. He is betting that personal diplomacy and deal volume can substitute for institutional checks, and that Washington and Beijing will accept a Kazakhstan that signs with both. The bet has held through the CPC strikes, the AI ultimatum and the sanctions bills.

## Constraints

- The system now depends on one person's judgement, with no successor or rival power centre.
- Russia controls the main oil route and can raise the cost of any pro-Western tilt.
- The EU and US condition deeper engagement on political reform that Tokayev has not delivered.
