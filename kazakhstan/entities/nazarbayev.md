# Nursultan Nazarbayev

Nazarbayev built the Kazakh state and its resource economy, and the system he created still sets the terms of Tokayev's choices. He is no longer a decision-maker, but the party, the security services, the state holding company and the informal networks he assembled remain the scaffolding of Kazakh politics.

## Key Actions

- **December 1991:** Became president after independence and began opening the oil sector to Chevron, ExxonMobil and Shell.
- **1993-2003:** Signed the first foreign oil deals and the early pipeline agreements with China that became the basis of the Belt and Road corridor.
- **2019:** Resigned as president while retaining the Security Council chair and the title "Leader of the Nation".
- **January 2022:** Was removed from those posts after the fuel-price protests, ending his formal influence.
- **August 2026:** Tokayev's amendments to the Foreign Policy Concept removed the last references to his formal role.

## Current Position

Nazarbayev's legacy is now contested. The government presents the 2026 constitutional changes as a break with the "Elbasy" system, but the institutions he built — Samruk-Kazyna, the ruling party, the security apparatus — are the same ones executing the current strategy. His economic model, built on Western oil majors and multi-vector diplomacy, remains the template.

## Constraints

- He holds no office and has no formal channel to influence policy.
- His family's business interests are exposed to the anti-corruption campaigns of the post-2022 period.
- His record is identified with the authoritarian system that Western partners criticise.
