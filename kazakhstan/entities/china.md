# China

China is Kazakhstan's largest trading partner and its most important source of non-Western capital. It buys oil, gas, uranium and grain, finances rail and border infrastructure, and now owns about a quarter of national oil production through CNPC and Sinopec. Beijing's goal is a stable western corridor for energy and freight; Astana's is to use Chinese money without becoming dependent on it.

## Key Actions

- **May 2026:** A joint China-Kazakhstan grain-trading platform opened in Astana, alongside plans to expand agricultural exports.
- **June 2026:** The two countries signed a civil nuclear protocol and selected CNNC to build two reactors; the first joint working group met in Astana.
- **July 2026:** Tokayev's Shanghai visit produced more than 70 agreements worth over $15 billion and launched the China-led WAICO.
- **August 2026:** Chinese and Kazakh officials reaffirmed a $100 billion trade target and agreed to a third cross-border railway crossing.
- **September 2026:** The Kazakhstan-China Investment Forum in Almaty signed 50 documents worth $8.2 billion.

## Current Position

China has moved from buying resources to building the plants and platforms that process them. Trade reached $32.4 billion in January-July 2026, up 24%, and more than 9,000 Chinese-backed companies operate in the country. The relationship is broadening into nuclear, AI, automotive assembly and payments, which gives Beijing more leverage than the oil trade alone ever did.

## Constraints

- China's own economy is slowing, and its banks are more selective than during the BRI boom.
- Kazakhstan's export bans and local-content rules limit how much of the value chain Beijing can keep.
- US tariff-evasion scrutiny and secondary sanctions raise the cost of routing trade through Kazakhstan.
