# Armenia-Azerbaijan Peace Process: Structural Notes

> Recent developments are tracked in `timeline/armenia-azerbaijan-timeline.md` at month resolution. This file records structural context and recurring dynamics, not a chronology.

## The Sequential Condition Chain (as of November 2026)

The peace process between Armenia and Azerbaijan operates on an explicit sequential condition model, publicly outlined by Azerbaijani Ambassador to Turkey Rashad Mammadov (May 19, 2026):

```
Constitutional amendment (remove territorial claims)
    → Peace agreement signing (initialed Aug 2025, not yet formally signed)
        → Border openings (Turkey-Armenia AND Armenia-Azerbaijan simultaneously)
```

**Key implications:**
- Azerbaijan's primary blocker is Armenia's constitution, not the peace treaty text itself
- Turkey explicitly links its border opening to the Armenia-Azerbaijan peace deal (not independent of it)
- The sequence means even if Armenia amends its constitution, the peace treaty must be signed before borders open
- Pashinyan has pledged constitutional changes, but a referendum is expected only after the June 2026 elections

## Domestic Constraints (Armenia)

- Pashinyan's Civil Contract won 49.81% of parliamentary seats (June 7, 2026 election) — majority but short of the 2/3 supermajority needed for constitutional reform
- Opposition: Strong Armenia (Samvel Karapetyan) 23.29%, Armenia alliance (Kocharyan) 9.9%, Prosperous Armenia 4%. Total opposition + pro-Russian bloc ~37%
- Karapetyan is under house arrest in Russia; called elections "shameful." Turnout: 58%
- Pashinyan's narrower mandate makes a constitutional referendum harder, but Carnegie analysts argue this makes a visible "peace dividend" more urgent, not a reason to delay
- The constitutional draft was finalized in August 2026 and is under review within the ruling party, but has not been published — constraining informed public debate
- Constitutional referendum expected later in 2026 or 2027; Pashinyan has framed it as the start of Armenia's "Fourth Republic" and has not ruled out a repeat referendum if the first fails
- A draft constitution is expected to be published by end-2026; after publication the path runs through a two-thirds parliamentary vote (which Civil Contract lacks) and then the referendum itself — so Pashinyan has floated snap elections as a route to the missing seats
- As of November 2026 every major opposition leader is in pretrial detention: Samvel Karapetyan (detained over ֏65bn alleged damage at Electric Networks of Armenia), Robert Kocharyan, and Gagik Tsarukyan. Opposition parties responded by forming a "pan-national consolidation" against the draft constitution
- All Constitutional Court judges have now been appointed under Pashinyan, including its new president Vladimir Vardanyan (6 November 2026), a Civil Contract MP until 2026 — relevant because the court reviews the amendments

## Aliyev's Escalating Preconditions (June 2026)

Post-election, Aliyev has added several new demands beyond constitutional change:
- **Resettlement of 300,000 Azerbaijanis** in Armenia under the "Western Azerbaijan" campaign
- **Return of former Azerbaijani enclaves** within Armenia
- **Territorial adjustments** via border "delimitation and demarcation"
- Azerbaijan formed the "Community of Western Azerbaijan" organization and circulated appeals to all UN member states (January and March 2023)
- A "Western Azerbaijan" conference was held in the US Congress halls on June 24, 2026
- Analysts assess Aliyev is in no rush to sign a peace deal, pressing for additional concessions as Pashinyan accepts existing demands

## Treaty and Border Timelines

- Deputy Foreign Minister Vahan Kostanyan announced in Brussels (June 24, 2026) a **2030** target for full border reopening with Turkey and Azerbaijan; certain transport routes could operationalize earlier
- Pashinyan set **2031** as his target for signing and ratifying the peace treaty (October 15, 2026), calling it his "most important task"
- Mirzoyan publicly contradicted the slow timeline on **October 28, 2026**, saying Armenia is ready to sign by end-2026 or in 2027 and rejecting claims of deliberate delay. The gap between the FM's readiness language and the PM's 2031 ceiling is a recurring feature — treat 2031 as a political floor, not a schedule
- Mirzoyan also confirmed Baku does not formally table the constitution as a negotiating demand, but has stated it must be resolved before initialling and signing. That is the operative sequencing claim to test against Azerbaijani statements
- Pashinyan proposed a mutual enclave exchange — Karki, Barkhudarlyu and Yukhari Askipara for Artsvashen — under a "no enclaves or exclaves on either side" formula; Mirzoyan said enclaves "do not pose an obstacle" to peace, leaving the constitution as the main blocker

## Key Railway/Transport Developments

- **Akhalkalaki-Kars railway (Georgia-Turkey):** Opened for Armenian trade May 24, 2026. Built as part of the Baku-Tbilisi-Kars project. 105 km total (76 km Turkey, 29 km Georgia).
- **Kars-Gyumri railway (direct Turkey-Armenia):** Idle since 1993. Repair work announced by Pashinyan. This is the only rail link directly between Turkey and Armenia.
- **Yeraskh junction:** Under Russian concession management. Pashinyan announced Armenia will repair it anyway.
- **Azerbaijan rail transit to Armenia:** Opened October 2025. By May 2026: 100K tons of grain, 8.5K tons diesel, ~4K tons gasoline transited.
- **TRIPP corridor:** US-Armenia agreement signed May 2026. DFC implementation structure: TDC US (wholly DFC-owned Delaware corp) and TDC (bi-national JV in Armenia, 74% DFC / 26% Armenia). Multimodal scope includes rail, road, oil/gas pipeline, electricity, fiberoptic. World Bank projections: ~$100M/year transport savings, 0.5-1.0% GDP growth, ~3% export growth, thousands of jobs. No DFC funding committed yet — sub-project equity capped at 40%. Construction is now targeted for 2027 (Bayramov, September 15, 2026), and Yerevan has ruled out any foreign military or security role on Armenian soil.
- **Ratified 28 October 2026** by 63 votes to 32, only the ruling faction in favour. The Armenian section is 42–43 km. Two trilateral technical planning groups (transport, electricity) were launched by joint US-Armenia-Azerbaijan statement on 6 November 2026. Domestic criticism centres on sequencing: Azerbaijan gets its Nakhchivan link before the peace treaty is signed.
- **Russian railway concession:** South Caucasus Railway (Russian Railways subsidiary) holds a 30-year concession from 2008, running to 2038. Freight traffic has halved. Pashinyan says Armenia will repair the Gyumri-Akhurik and Yeraskh sections itself; in October 2026 the two sides agreed a three-month joint working group on Russian investment, with Moscow still refusing either to invest or to transfer the concession.

## Russia's Economic Leverage

- **Energy:** Russia supplied ~82% of Armenia's gas in 2025. Gazprom Armenia suspended supplies for ten days in September 2026 — a shift from trade restrictions to energy pressure.
- **Trade:** Russia banned Armenian fish and seafood imports in June 2026 and water and soft drinks in August 2026.
- **Political:** Moscow set a December 2026 deadline for an EU/EAEU referendum and outlined a procedure to suspend Armenia's CSTO voting rights; Medvedev warned in October 2026 that Armenia could become "a new Ukraine."
- **Armenian response:** Pashinyan says relations with Russia "can no longer be the same"; the 2026-2031 government programme dropped the description of Russia as a "strategic alliance" for the first time since 2018, and a comprehensive review now covers transport, energy, CSTO membership and the 102nd military base.
- **CSTO rupture, November 2026:** Mirzoyan ruled out any return to the CSTO (28 Oct); Pashinyan declared Armenia's CSTO interests "exhausted" (5 Nov) while confirming Armenia stays in the EAEU. A Moscow Collective Security Council session on 11 November was to consider suspending Armenia's voting rights and barring Armenian nominees from posts, over unpaid dues — note the distinction: suspension of rights is not expulsion, which Russia says is Yerevan's own call.
- **EU trajectory:** Pashinyan now names 2027 or 2028 for a formal EU membership application, while insisting no referendum is possible yet because there is no concrete offer to vote on.

## Military Doctrine Shift

As of Republic Day 2026, Pashinyan announced:
- Armenian army's sole mission is defense of internationally recognized territory
- No operations outside borders except peacekeeping
- Equipment from 7 countries (up from near-zero before 2022)
- Defense industry: 170B drams invested since 2022
- Fortified border zones constructed along Armenian borders
