# Nikol Pashinyan

Pashinyan has staked his mandate on a peace treaty with Azerbaijan and a pivot away from Russia. The constitutional amendments Baku demands are his to deliver, but his party lacks the two-thirds majority to put them to a referendum.

## Key Actions
- **June 2026:** Civil Contract wins 49.8% — a majority, but short of two-thirds — in an election marked by documented Russian interference.
- **July 2026:** The government approves the TRIPP framework bill and sends it to the Constitutional Court, creating a development company on a 49-year lease.
- **August 2026:** A new government programme downgrades relations with Russia, and Pashinyan meets Aliyev in Bishkek — their first contact since the election.
- **September 2026:** He rejects Putin's demand for an immediate EU referendum, calls the Russian 102nd base "not vital", and meets Erdoğan at the UN General Assembly.
- **October 2026:** He sets 2031 as his target for signing and ratifying a treaty and proposes a mutual enclave exchange; parliament ratifies TRIPP 63-32.
- **November 2026:** He says Armenia could apply for EU membership in 2027-28, declares CSTO interests "exhausted", and detains every major opposition leader ahead of the constitutional fight.

## Current Position
Pashinyan has consolidated control: the Constitutional Court is now entirely staffed by his appointees, the opposition is in pretrial detention, and he has floated snap elections to win the two-thirds he needs. He keeps Armenia in the EAEU while abandoning the CSTO, and his timetable for a treaty (2031) is slower than his foreign minister's (end-2026 or 2027). Both agree Baku will not sign before the constitution changes.

## Constraints
- Baku's preconditions go beyond the constitution: enclaves, border delimitation and the "Western Azerbaijan" claims.
- Russia still controls gas supplies, trade access and the railway concession until 2038.
- Detaining the opposition raises the cost of the referendum he needs to win.
