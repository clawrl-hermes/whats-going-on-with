# Recep Tayyip Erdoğan

## Relevance to Armenia-Azerbaijan
Turkey is Azerbaijan's closest strategic ally and was instrumental in Azerbaijan's 2020 military victory in Nagorno-Karabakh. Turkey shut its border with Armenia in 1993 in solidarity with Azerbaijan during the first war.

## Normalization with Armenia
In 2026, Turkey took concrete steps toward normalizing relations with Armenia:
- **May 11, 2026:** Turkey lifted restrictions on direct trade declarations with Armenia after 33 years
- **June 3, 2026:** Historic Armenia-Turkey business forum held in Kars
- Railway connections through Georgia reopened for Armenian imports/exports

Turkey's normalization is driven by the new power reality after Azerbaijan's victory — Ankara can afford to be flexible toward Yerevan without jeopardizing its partnership with Baku.

## Key Constraint
The border remains closed. Full diplomatic relations have not been restored. Turkey's normalization is conditional on Armenia's peace process with Azerbaijan and is closely coordinated with Baku.

## Border Status (November 2026)

Mirzoyan said on 5 November that the border "could open at any time," noting the Margara crossing has been renovated and is technically ready; a Turkish-Armenian business forum in Kars has already taken place, with the next one due in Armenia in early 2027. Yet Ankara still formally ties full normalisation to an Armenia-Azerbaijan peace treaty, and goods continue to move through Georgia.

The key analytical counterweight is Alexander Iskandaryan's argument (JAMnews, November 2026) that the odds of an early opening are "quite low": Turkish policy toward Armenia is rational political calculation rather than sentiment, the BTK railway through Georgia remains underused so the economic case for a new route is weak, and the visible Ankara-Baku rift — differing approaches to Israel, Northern Cyprus, and the school hijab ban — does not mean Turkey will stop listening to Baku. Opening the border, he adds, would not by itself constitute normalisation.

## Turkey-Azerbaijan Rift (2026)

The school hijab ban dispute, Baku's Israel ties and Turkey's walkout over President Herzog's CICA speech exposed the sharpest public divergence in years. Erdoğan was due in Baku on 8 November 2026 to repair ties, with Ankara reportedly offering concessions on religious schooling while Baku pressed defence and Israel-related grievances.
