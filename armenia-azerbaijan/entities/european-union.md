# European Union

The EU has become Armenia's principal alternative to Russia in trade and political alignment, but it offers economics rather than security. Its support is the cushion that makes the break with Moscow survivable.

## Key Actions
- **June 2026:** Pashinyan announces von der Leyen will visit Yerevan to discuss agricultural exports, offsetting Russian import bans.
- **July 2026:** Von der Leyen visits and announces autonomous trade measures covering about 80% of Armenian exports, plus the final €18M tranche of a €52M package; she calls Russian pressure "economic violence".
- **October 2026:** The trade measures take effect, opening duty-free access for most Armenian agricultural goods.
- **November 2026:** Pashinyan says Armenia could formally apply for membership in 2027 or 2028, while staying in the EAEU.

## Current Position
Brussels is deepening trade and connectivity support — including a "Connectivity for Peace" package of up to €200M — and is preparing for a possible membership application. It has no appetite for a fast accession, and Mirzoyan concedes Armenia has no membership timetable. Visa liberalisation is a live but slow track.

## Constraints
- The EU will not provide security guarantees, which leaves Armenia dependent on other partners for defence.
- Member-state unanimity and enlargement fatigue limit how fast any of this can move.
- Too visible an EU embrace gives Moscow a pretext for further economic pressure.
