# Islamic Republic of Iran

Iran shares a border with Armenia and has become a balancing partner for Yerevan as relations with Russia sour. It supplies gas through a swap-for-electricity arrangement and sells arms, but its central interest is that the Syunik corridor does not become a US or Azerbaijani foothold on its northern border.

## Key Interests
- **No foreign presence:** Tehran calls concerns about US infrastructure near its border "legitimate and logical" and keeps TRIPP risk management on the bilateral agenda.
- **Territorial red line:** Iran opposes any Azerbaijani attempt to take territory in Syunik, which it treats as a buffer.
- **Transit:** It wants to preserve its own north-south route to Armenia and the Black Sea, and is wary of a corridor that bypasses it.
- **Energy:** Iran is pushing a new trade model to increase gas exports to Armenia beyond the current swap.

## Key Actions
- **May 2026:** Armenia displays Iran's AD-08 Majid air-defence system at its Republic Day parade.
- **July 2026:** Tehran and Yerevan prepare a strategic partnership agreement; Iran frames it as a defensive shield against US encirclement.
- **July 2026:** Iran lists Azerbaijan's AzTV as "enemy media"; Baku bans Iranian outlets in return.
- **Late 2026:** After Mirzoyan's Tehran visit, he tells parliament Iran is "satisfied" with the TRIPP process and Armenia's transparency.

## Current Position
Iran has moved from outright opposition to conditional acceptance, provided it is consulted and the corridor does not host foreign forces. Its relations with Baku remain tense over the 2026 war and media bans, which gives Yerevan room to play both sides. The limit is structural: Iran cannot match Russia's economic leverage or the West's investment.
