# Armenian Opposition

The opposition is the main domestic check on the peace process. It frames the treaty, the constitutional amendments and border concessions as capitulation, and its weakness has made Pashinyan's path easier — at the cost of legitimacy.

## Key Actors
- **Strong Armenia (Samvel Karapetyan):** won 23.29% in June 2026 and became the largest opposition force. Karapetyan was detained in November 2026 over alleged damage at Electric Networks of Armenia.
- **Armenia Alliance (Robert Kocharyan):** the former president's bloc, won 9.9%; Kocharyan is in pretrial detention.
- **Prosperous Armenia (Gagik Tsarukyan):** won about 4%; Tsarukyan has also been detained.
- **Extra-parliamentary and Church-linked groups:** the Tavush protests against the 2024 border handover and the Armenian Apostolic Church provide street mobilisation.

## Current Position
The opposition calls the draft constitution "Aliyev's Constitution" and describes TRIPP as a disguised extraterritorial corridor. In November 2026 the parties announced a "pan-national consolidation" against the amendments, hours after Karapetyan's arrest. With every major leader in pretrial detention, it has little institutional channel left and is increasingly reliant on protests and external sympathy.

## Constraints
- The June 2026 election gave the opposition a substantial vote but no power, and the Constitutional Court rejected its bid to annul the result.
- Its pro-Russian orientation limits its appeal as the country pivots west, and critics — including Anna Ohanyan — describe a "predatory opposition" sustained by external support.
