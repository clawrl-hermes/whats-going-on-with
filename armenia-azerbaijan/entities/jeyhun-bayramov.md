# Jeyhun Bayramov

Bayramov carries Baku's terms in public: the constitution first, delimitation on Azerbaijani terms, and TRIPP on a slower timetable than Washington or Yerevan would like. He is the counterpart Mirzoyan meets when the process needs a visible step.

## Key Actions
- **July 2026:** Visits Moscow despite a Russian media campaign against Azerbaijan, keeping official dialogue open.
- **August 2026:** Makes the highest-level Azerbaijani visit to Kyiv since the full-scale war, backing Ukraine's territorial integrity.
- **September 2026:** Announces that delimitation will resume from the northern tripoint with Georgia and proceed south, and confirms TRIPP construction has slipped to 2027.
- **September 2026:** Meets Mirzoyan at the UN General Assembly and agrees to a "positive agenda" in multilateral platforms.
- **October 2026:** Reiterates that constitutional change is the prerequisite for a final peace agreement.

## Current Position
Bayramov describes the situation as "de facto peace" and frames the treaty as a formality once Armenia amends its constitution. He presents Azerbaijan as the victor that can afford to wait, and he keeps TRIPP at arm's length from the bilateral negotiation, describing it as primarily an Armenia-US project.

## Constraints
- The foreign ministry does not set the timetable; Aliyev does, and his congress speech in October hardened the tone.
- Baku's own commitments — resettlement, the "Western Azerbaijan" campaign, defence spending — shape how its demands are read abroad.
