This topic tracks the slow, contested move from war to a signed peace between Armenia and Azerbaijan. It follows the negotiations themselves — border delimitation, the constitutional question, transport corridors and the status of prisoners — alongside the external powers whose interests converge on the South Caucasus: Russia, Turkey, the United States, Iran and the European Union.

The through-line is Armenia's attempt to rebalance away from Russia without losing the security and economic ties it still depends on. Pashinyan's government has pursued a Western pivot, EU trade measures and a normalization track with Turkey, while Azerbaijan bargains from a position of military and territorial advantage and Russia uses energy, trade and its railway concession as leverage.

Coverage also extends to the corridor competition that now defines the region's infrastructure: the US-brokered TRIPP route through Syunik, Azerbaijan's Zangezur demands, the Middle Corridor, and the rail and road projects that would reconnect the South Caucasus to both Europe and Central Asia.
