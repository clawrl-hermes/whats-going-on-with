- **TRIPP becomes law in Armenia** — Parliament ratified the Armenia-US framework agreement 63–32 on 28 October, creating a development company that is 74% US-owned for an initial 49 years. Only the ruling Civil Contract faction voted in favour.
- **A trilateral structure finally exists** — On 6 November Washington, Yerevan and Baku announced two technical planning groups on transport and electricity transmission, the first formal three-way working mechanism since the August 2025 Washington declaration.
- **Mirzoyan moves the goalposts forward** — The foreign minister said on 28 October that Armenia is ready to sign by end-2026 or in 2027, far earlier than Pashinyan's 2031 target, while confirming Baku will not sign before constitutional change.
- **Pashinyan picks his constitutional fight** — His government detained Samvel Karapetyan and 35 associates over alleged damage at Electric Networks of Armenia, and the opposition answered with a "pan-national consolidation" against "Aliyev's Constitution". Every major opposition leader is now in pretrial detention.
- **The Constitutional Court is now fully Pashinyan's** — Former Civil Contract MP Vladimir Vardanyan was elected its president on 6 November, just as the court prepares to review the amendments Baku demands.
- **Russia's alliance is formally abandoned, its market is not** — Pashinyan said Armenia's CSTO interests are "exhausted" and Mirzoyan ruled out returning, days before a Moscow session on suspending Armenia's voting rights — but Armenia stays in the EAEU and now targets an EU application in 2027–2028.
- **The Turkey border is close and not close** — Mirzoyan says it could open "at any time" with Margara ready; Alexander Iskandaryan puts the odds "quite low", arguing the Ankara-Baku rift will not change Turkish policy.
