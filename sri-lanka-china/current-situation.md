Sri Lanka has completed its debt restructuring and returned to growth, but the recovery now depends on two things it does not fully control: the IMF board and Chinese capital. The seventh review reached staff-level agreement on 4 October, unlocking about $345 million once the board approves, while reserves slipped to $6.85 billion at end-September against an $8 billion year-end target. Fitch upgraded the country to B- and the World Bank put GDP back at pre-crisis levels, yet poverty remains at 16.9%. The most recent decision to change the picture was China Merchants' withdrawal from the $392 million Colombo logistics hub, which leaves the SLPA seeking a PPP partner even as a Chinese joint venture won the Sampur transmission line and the Kolonnawa battery contract.

## The Open Question

Can Colombo close the seventh review and reach the $8 billion reserves target before the current IMF programme expires in March 2027, while Chinese investment shifts from ports to grid and energy?
