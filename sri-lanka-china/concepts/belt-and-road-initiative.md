# Belt and Road Initiative in Sri Lanka

## Overview

Sri Lanka was an early Belt and Road node, positioned on the Maritime Silk Road and financed almost entirely by two Chinese policy banks: China Exim Bank for concessional and preferential buyer's credit, and China Development Bank for the Port City equity-style investment. Cumulative Chinese infrastructure commitments between 2006 and 2019 reached about $12.1 billion. Almost none of it was new after 2019.

## The Portfolio

- **Norochcholai coal power plant (2006, $1.35bn, Exim Bank).** Sri Lanka's first coal station; operational, with persistent environmental and cost criticisms.
- **Hambantota port (2007 and 2012, $307m and $390m, Exim Bank).** Two phases, leased to China Merchants in 2017; now a working container port.
- **Colombo-Katunayake Expressway (2009, $248m, Exim Bank).** Operational and tolled.
- **Mattala airport (2010, $191m, Exim Bank).** Operational but underused; a 30-year BOT tender was issued in 2026.
- **Colombo Port City (2012, $1.4bn, China Development Bank).** Reclaimed land and a special economic zone; suspended in 2016, relaunched in 2017, now home to more than 150 licensed companies.
- **Lotus Tower (2012, $104m, Exim Bank).** Operational.
- **Central Expressway Section I (2019, $989m committed, Exim Bank).** Only $51.5 million disbursed before the crisis; revived in 2026 with a $500 million renminbi loan.

## How It Was Financed

Most projects used Exim Bank preferential buyer's credit, lent to the government and tied to a Chinese contractor, usually China Harbour Engineering or China Machinery Engineering. The first Hambantota loan carried a commercial 6.4% rate; later tranches were concessional at about 2%. Port City was structured differently, as a development-bank investment in reclaimed land rather than a sovereign loan. None of the loan agreements contained asset-seizure clauses or used the assets as collateral.

## What Survived

The crisis did not cancel the portfolio; it froze it. Hambantota and Port City kept operating, the expressway was completed, and the remaining Chinese lending moved to finishing existing projects. New BRI engagement stopped: the Green Finance and Development Center recorded no new Chinese BRI investment or construction contracts in Sri Lanka in the first half of 2026. Beijing substituted small grants, including RMB79 million for cyclone-damaged bridges, for new sovereign credit.

## Current Status

The BRI in Sri Lanka is now a maintenance portfolio rather than an expansion programme. The assets are built, the debt is restructured, and the political contest has moved to how they are governed and who operates them. The one live expansion is Hambantota's container capacity, driven by commercial traffic rather than a new bilateral package.
