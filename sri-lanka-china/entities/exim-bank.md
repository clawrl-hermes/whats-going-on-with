# Exim Bank

The Export-Import Bank of China is the single most important external financier in Sri Lanka's modern infrastructure build-out. It financed the coal plant, the expressways, the airport and both phases of Hambantota, then became one of the two Chinese policy banks that restructured Sri Lanka's bilateral debt after the 2022 default. Its shift to renminbi lending is the clearest signal of how its lending model is changing.

## Key Actions

- **2006:** Lent $1.35 billion for the Norochcholai coal power station, one of Sri Lanka's largest Chinese loans.
- **2007-2012:** Financed Hambantota Phase I and II, the Colombo-Katunayake Expressway, Mattala airport and the Lotus Tower.
- **2019:** Committed $989 million to Section I of the Central Expressway; only $51.5 million was disbursed before the crisis.
- **June 2024:** Reached agreement with Colombo on bilateral debt treatment alongside the Official Creditor Committee.
- **September 2026:** Approved a $500 million renminbi-denominated loan for the Central Expressway, Sri Lanka's first yuan bilateral credit, after cutting its original commitment.

## Current Position

Exim Bank remains Sri Lanka's largest bilateral creditor, with about $5.01 billion outstanding in mid-2026. Its new lending is smaller, project-specific and increasingly denominated in renminbi, a shift AidData reported across several sovereign borrowers. The bank is no longer financing prestige projects at pre-crisis scale; it is finishing projects it already started and protecting the value of loans already on its books.

## Constraints

- Its exposure is concentrated in a borrower that defaulted once and still faces large repayments from 2028.
- Renminbi lending transfers currency risk to Sri Lanka, which earns almost nothing in yuan.
- Its lending is tied to Chinese contractors, which limits competitive tendering and raises costs.
