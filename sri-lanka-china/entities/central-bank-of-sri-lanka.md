# Central Bank of Sri Lanka

The Central Bank of Sri Lanka manages the currency, foreign reserves and interest rates, and was the institution through which the 2022 crisis was felt most directly. It ran out of usable foreign exchange, watched the rupee collapse, and then administered the stabilisation that followed. It is now rebuilding reserves while managing the currency risk created by renminbi borrowing.

## Key Actions

- **2022:** Reserves fell to $1.9 billion with usable foreign exchange near $50 million; inflation passed 70%.
- **2023-2025:** Tightened policy, supported the IMF programme and oversaw the gradual return of exchange-rate flexibility.
- **2026:** Raised the policy rate by 100 basis points after the Middle East war pushed inflation to 5.5%, then held as the shock faded.
- **August 2026:** Reserves stood at $6.5 billion, supported by multilateral disbursements and remittances.
- **September 2026:** Reserves fell 1.1% to $6.851 billion; the bank continued net dollar purchases ahead of the 2028 bond repayments.

## Current Position

The bank has rebuilt a usable buffer and restored a functioning monetary framework, but it remains exposed to external shocks and to the fiscal authority's borrowing needs. Its reserves are strong enough to smooth the currency, not strong enough to defend it against a sustained oil shock. The shift to yuan-denominated borrowing adds a conversion obligation the bank will have to manage.

## Constraints

- It must service large external repayments from 2028 without exhausting reserves.
- Its policy rate is constrained by the IMF programme and by the government's growth target.
- It cannot print foreign exchange, and the rupee is still not fully flexible.
