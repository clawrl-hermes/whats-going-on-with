# China

China is Sri Lanka's largest bilateral creditor and the external actor that has shaped its infrastructure most deeply. The relationship began with Rajapaksa-era borrowing after Western lenders declined, peaked with the Belt and Road build-out, and was forced into restructuring after the 2022 default. It is now being rebuilt on smaller, more commercial terms.

## Key Actions

- **2005-2019:** Committed about $12.1 billion to Sri Lankan infrastructure, from Norochcholai and Hambantota to Colombo Port City.
- **January 2025:** Hosted Dissanayake in Beijing; a joint statement reaffirmed cooperation on Hambantota, Port City, logistics and the digital economy.
- **March 2026:** Sri Lanka refused entry to the research vessel Shi Yan 6 and paused Chinese naval visits, and Beijing did not retaliate.
- **June 2026:** Deepened party-to-party ties and hosted a 250-strong Sri Lankan trade delegation at the China-South Asia Expo.
- **August-September 2026:** Replaced its ambassador, renewed the push for a free trade agreement, and sent a trade minister to the Belt and Road Summit in Hong Kong.

## Current Position

Beijing still has more money and more infrastructure exposure in Sri Lanka than any other bilateral partner, but it is no longer the default financier. It is substituting grants and trade for sovereign loans, courting the NPP government through party channels, and waiting for the 2027 anniversary year to conclude a free trade agreement. Its influence is real but bounded by India's security role and by Colombo's IMF commitments.

## Constraints

- It cannot resume large-scale lending without worsening a debt stock it has just restructured.
- India and the United States now have security relationships with Colombo that China cannot match.
- Its free trade offer is unpopular with Sri Lankan manufacturers, who fear cheaper Chinese imports.
