# Dissanayake Administration

Anura Kumara Dissanayake's National People's Power government took office in November 2024 with 159 of 225 seats and a mandate to break with the Rajapaksa-era political order. It has kept the IMF programme, maintained Chinese infrastructure cooperation and deepened ties with India, while presenting itself as a cleaner and more accountable government. The balancing act is the defining feature of its foreign economic policy.

## Key Actions

- **November 2024:** Won the snap parliamentary election; Dissanayake became president.
- **January 2025:** Visited Beijing and reaffirmed BRI cooperation in Hambantota, Port City, logistics and the digital economy.
- **March 2026:** Refused entry to the Chinese research vessel Shi Yan 6 and paused Chinese naval visits.
- **September 2026:** Secured cabinet approval for the $500 million yuan-denominated Exim Bank loan and signed the cyclone bridge grant agreement.
- **2026:** Kept the IMF programme on track, completed the fifth and sixth reviews, and began talks on a successor arrangement.

## Current Position

The government has preserved the recovery without abandoning either great-power relationship. It is more comfortable with Indian and Western engagement than its predecessor, but it has not reversed any major Chinese project and continues to court Chinese trade and tourism. Its legitimacy depends on delivering growth and clean government before the 2028 debt repayments test the budget.

## Constraints

- It inherited an IMF programme that limits subsidies and spending.
- Its parliamentary majority is large but its coalition includes parties with different views on China.
- The north and east expect a peace-and-development dividend that the central budget cannot yet fund.
