# India

India is the external actor with the most direct interest in limiting Chinese influence in Sri Lanka and the most capacity to act on it. It provided emergency financing during the 2022 crisis, operates a container terminal in Colombo, and is building a presence in energy and shipbuilding. Its footprint is smaller than China's in dollars but more dispersed and strategically targeted.

## Key Actions

- **2022:** Provided about $4 billion in emergency financing during the default crisis, more than any other bilateral partner.
- **2025:** Signed its first formal defence cooperation framework with Sri Lanka and pushed Indian investment above $2 billion.
- **April 2026:** Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders completed a 51% acquisition of Colombo Dockyard.
- **2026:** Advanced the India-UAE-Sri Lanka plan to develop Trincomalee as an energy hub, and provided $450 million in Cyclone Ditwah relief.
- **November 2026:** Colombo appointed a committee to expand the India-Sri Lanka free trade agreement.

## Current Position

India is now embedded in Sri Lanka's ports, shipbuilding, energy and defence in a way it was not a decade ago. It has also learned to work through multilateral formats, including the Trincomalee trilateral, that are harder for Colombo to reject as bilateral pressure. Its influence is strongest in the north and east, where Chinese investment is thin.

## Constraints

- Sri Lanka's trade deficit with India is large, and Indian firms have a mixed record on project delivery.
- Delhi cannot match Chinese financing volumes for large infrastructure.
- Its security cooperation is sensitive in a country that prizes non-alignment.
