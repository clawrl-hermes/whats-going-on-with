# China Merchants Port Holdings (CMPort)

CMPort is China's largest port operator and the commercial face of Chinese involvement in Sri Lankan ports. It operates the Colombo International Container Terminal, holds the Hambantota lease through a joint venture, and has become the most important single investor in the country's maritime infrastructure. Its decisions now shape both ports' traffic.

## Key Actions

- **2011:** Entered a joint venture to operate the Colombo International Container Terminal, which became profitable within a year.
- **2017:** Acquired a 70% stake and a 99-year lease in Hambantota for $1.12 billion, with Sri Lanka retaining 30%.
- **2025:** Benefited from MSC's decision to shift transshipment calls to Hambantota, lifting volumes from 53,170 to 428,036 TEU.
- **July 2026:** Began talks with MSC over a stake in Hambantota; the discussions remained unresolved.
- **October 2026:** Withdrew from the $392 million South Asia Commercial and Logistics Hub in Colombo, citing changed commercial circumstances.

## Current Position

CMPort is expanding Hambantota while retreating from Colombo logistics. It committed $108 million to cranes and yard equipment and studied a second phase of container berths as volumes crossed one million TEU. The SACLH exit signals that it is reassessing Sri Lankan risk even as its existing port asset performs better than at any point since the lease.

## Constraints

- Hambantota's growth depends heavily on MSC, which is negotiating to become a shareholder.
- The 99-year lease is politically contested and cannot be used as a foreign military base.
- CMPort's investment decisions are commercial, but they are read strategically by India and the United States.
