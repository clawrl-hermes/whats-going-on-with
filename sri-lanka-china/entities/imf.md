# International Monetary Fund

The IMF is the external anchor of Sri Lanka's recovery. Its $2.9 billion Extended Fund Facility, approved in March 2023, was the condition for debt restructuring and the template for fiscal policy. The programme expires in March 2027, and the government is already negotiating a successor arrangement that would shift from crisis support to growth.

## Key Actions

- **March 2023:** Approved the 48-month, $2.9 billion EFF, conditional on debt restructuring and fiscal consolidation.
- **May 2026:** Completed the combined fifth and sixth reviews, releasing about $695 million and bringing total access to roughly $2.4 billion.
- **June 2026:** A staff visit warned that the Middle East war had pushed inflation from 1.6% to 5.5% and endorsed the central bank's 100-basis-point rate hike.
- **October 2026:** Reached staff-level agreement on the seventh review, unlocking about $345 million, subject to the 2027 budget.
- **2026:** Began discussions with Colombo on a post-EFF programme to start after March 2027.

## Current Position

The programme has restored fiscal credibility and unlocked multilateral financing, but it has also constrained the government's room to cushion fuel and electricity prices. The seventh review tied disbursement to the 2027 budget, and the successor arrangement will shape how much fiscal space Colombo has when the 2028 bond repayments begin.

## Constraints

- Its conditions are politically costly in a country still recovering from an inflation shock.
- It depends on debt restructuring staying on track, including the Chinese bilateral track.
- It cannot compel other creditors, so delays in restructuring become delays in disbursement.
