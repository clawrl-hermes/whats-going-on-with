# Timeline: China-Sri Lanka Financial Relations

## 2005
- Mahinda Rajapaksa is elected president and turns to China for development finance after Western lenders prove reluctant during the civil war.

## 2007
- China Exim Bank provides $307 million for Phase 1 of the Hambantota deep-sea port, after India and Japan decline to finance it.

## 2009
- The civil war ends in May and reconstruction accelerates Chinese lending, including $248 million for the Colombo-Katunayake Expressway.

## 2010
- China Exim Bank funds Mattala Rajapaksa International Airport with a $191 million loan; the airport is widely criticised as a white elephant.

## 2011
- China Merchants Port Holdings enters a joint venture to operate the Colombo International Container Terminal.

## 2013
- China launches the Belt and Road Initiative and positions Sri Lanka as a node on the Maritime Silk Road.

## 2014
- Chinese submarines dock at Colombo twice despite Indian objections; Exim Bank provides $200 million for a Hambantota industrial zone.

## 2017
- China Merchants Port Holdings receives a 70% stake and a 99-year lease of Hambantota for $1.12 billion, above the port's valuation.

## 2020
- COVID-19 collapses tourism and remittances; foreign debt reaches $56.3 billion and the debt-to-GDP ratio hits 119%.

## 2022
- **January:**
  - Colombo formally asks China to reschedule its debt during Wang Yi's visit; Beijing gives no immediate response.
- **April:**
  - Sri Lanka suspends payments on about $46 billion of external debt, its first sovereign default.
- **July:**
  - Mass protests force Gotabaya Rajapaksa to flee; Ranil Wickremesinghe becomes president.
- **September:**
  - Formal restructuring talks begin with China, the Paris Club and bondholders.

## 2023
- **March:**
  - The IMF approves a $2.9 billion Extended Fund Facility, conditional on progress with debt restructuring.

## 2024
- **June:**
  - Sri Lanka reaches agreement with China Exim Bank and the Official Creditor Committee on bilateral debt terms.
- **September:**
  - Bondholders agree to restructuring with 98% participation.
- **November:**
  - The NPP wins a snap election and Anura Kumara Dissanayake becomes president.
- **December:**
  - The bondholder restructuring is finalised.

## 2025
- **January:**
  - Dissanayake visits Beijing; a joint statement reaffirms BRI cooperation in Hambantota, Colombo Port City, logistics and the digital economy.

## 2026
- **January:**
  - Wang Yi stops in Colombo; Sri Lanka says the $3.7 billion SINOPEC refinery agreement will be finalised in the first quarter.
- **March:**
  - Sri Lanka refuses entry to the Chinese research vessel Shi Yan 6 and pauses Chinese naval visits indefinitely.
- **April:**
  - India's Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders acquires a 51% stake in Colombo Dockyard.
- **May:**
  - The IMF completes the combined fifth and sixth reviews, releasing about $695 million.
  - Sri Lanka issues a 30-year build-operate-transfer expression of interest for Mattala airport.
- **June:**
  - AidData reports that China Exim Bank is pushing yuan-denominated loans, with Sri Lanka among the borrowers.
  - Dissanayake meets CHEC chairman Bai Yinzhan and reaffirms Port City as a government priority.
- **July:**
  - The World Bank reclassifies Sri Lanka as an upper-middle-income economy.
  - MSC enters talks to acquire a stake in Hambantota and begins switching transshipment calls from Colombo.
  - Ambassador Qi Zhenhong ends his tenure and renews the call for a free trade agreement.
- **August:**
  - The Treasury reports 94% of public external debt restructuring implemented, with reserves at $6.5 billion.
  - China grants RMB79 million to rebuild 14 cyclone-damaged bridges.
  - Wei Huaxiang arrives as Chinese ambassador.
- **September:**
  - Cabinet approves a $500 million yuan-denominated Exim Bank loan for Central Expressway Section I.
  - CMPort studies expanding Hambantota capacity as volumes surge; MSC stake talks remain unresolved.
  - China and Sri Lanka sign the implementation agreement for the bridge grant.
- **October:**
  - The IMF reaches staff-level agreement on the seventh review, unlocking about $345 million.
  - CMPort withdraws from the $392 million Colombo logistics hub.
  - Foreign reserves fall to $6.85 billion at end-September.
- **November:**
  - A Ceylex-Xingneng joint venture wins the AIIB-funded Sampur-Kappalthurai transmission contract.
  - Sri Lanka appoints a committee to expand its free trade agreement with India.
