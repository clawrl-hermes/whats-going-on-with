- **The IMF Board now sets the clock at 2 December, with about $345 million and a resuming debt-service hump behind it** — the Executive Board will take up the Article IV consultation and seventh EFF review that day, alongside waivers and a financing assurances review; foreign debt service is put at nearly $3 billion in 2027 and $3.6 billion in 2028 as payments to bondholders and bilateral creditors including China, Japan and India restart ([Daily Mirror](https://www.dailymirror.lk/breaking-news/IMF-to-consider-Sri-Lankas-seventh-programme-review-on-December-2/108-355503)).
- **The trade relationship is thinning even as the diplomatic one warms: the 2025 deficit with China reached $4.87 billion** — exports of $278.71 million against imports of about $5.15 billion, with Chinese imports up roughly 28% from $4.3 billion in 2024, leaving China as 24.06% of Sri Lanka's import bill but just 2.14% of its exports ([The Morning](https://www.themorning.lk/articles/ygixfyKiBR3Jq7HJpxnI)).
- **Colombo is widening market access at the margin rather than at the tariff line** — protocols are now in place for cinnamon, seafood, pineapple, wheat bran pellets and poultry (farmed fish and mangoes pending), and a CCPIT-backed Trusted Trader certification is meant to strip the sovereign-rating discount out of Chinese commercial credit for exporters and SME importers ([The Morning](https://www.themorning.lk/articles/ygixfyKiBR3Jq7HJpxnI)).
- **RCEP accession has moved from an application to a formal evaluation** — Foreign Minister Vijitha Herath told Parliament that RCEP has appointed a dedicated committee to assess Sri Lanka's bid, and that a cabinet committee reviewing existing FTAs has reported, with sub-committees to decide next steps and revised agreements to be implemented from early next year ([Daily Mirror](https://www.dailymirror.lk/breaking-news/Sri-Lanka-hopeful-of-securing-RCEP-membership-Foreign-Minister/108-355577)).
- **Beijing's public line has shifted from projects to paper: its ambassador is now pressing for an accelerated FTA and an "upgrade" of Belt and Road cooperation** — the op-ed in Colombo's business daily pairs the FTA push with an assurance that Chinese cooperation carries no political conditions, published as the Sinopec refinery deal heads for cancellation and weeks after China Merchants' logistics-hub exit ([Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/columns/Seizing-new-opportunities-in-China-s-development-writing-a-new-chapter-in-China-Sri-Lanka-cooperation/4-800517)).
