- **2027 budget ties record spending to the IMF's board condition** — the Appropriation Bill sets spending at Rs9.92 trillion, net borrowing at Rs3.8 trillion and a record Rs2 trillion capital allocation, with primary expenditure capped at 12.9% of GDP. The presentation was the condition the IMF attached to board approval of the seventh review, but capital budget utilisation of 64%, 54% and 76% in 2023–25 weakens the growth case ([EconomyNext](https://economynext.com/preview-sri-lanka-unveils-2027-budget-amid-oil-shocks-missing-mega-projects-eyes-on-next-imf-deal-289811)).
- **An 18th IMF programme becomes the working assumption** — President Dissanayake confirmed the Finance Ministry and Central Bank are evaluating modalities for an arrangement after the EFF expires in March 2027, with the fuel import bill already at $4.082 billion by August, above the whole of 2025 ([EconomyNext](https://economynext.com/preview-sri-lanka-unveils-2027-budget-amid-oil-shocks-missing-mega-projects-eyes-on-next-imf-deal-289811)).
- **Restructuring reaches 99% only once unsigned terms are counted** — the GSDR's seventh progress report puts completed restructuring at about 94% of debt in the perimeter and 99% including agreements in principle, with the SriLankan Airlines debt exchange as the latest step. Hamilton Reserve Bank litigation is still unresolved, with final arguments filed and no ruling date set ([News 1st](https://english.newsfirst.lk/2026/10/15/sri-lanka-s-debt-crisis-nears-end-as-99-of-restructuring-is-finalized)).
- **China has become a net collector while multilateral lenders fund the recovery** — the IMF and ADB supplied nearly 80% of the roughly $1.48 billion in development financing Sri Lanka received in 2025, and the ADB raised its 2026 allocation to $480 million after Cyclone Ditwah, as Beijing now takes in more from developing countries than it lends ([The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2026/11/asias-debt-handoff-who-finances-the-post-bri-era)).
- **Beijing's own documents overstate its multilateral role** — Chinese official guidance describes a single "$10 billion" restructuring with the creditor committee and Exim Bank, but Exim Bank settled separately in October 2023 as an observer outside the committee, an arrangement that gave China speed rather than harsher terms ([ISDP](https://www.isdp.eu/publication/what-does-china-actually-think-about-sri-lanka)).
- **Yunnan opens a provincial trade channel while the FTA stays stalled** — a 23 October MoU gives Sri Lankan exporters free warehousing, exhibition booths, online market access and customs facilitation, plus a letter of intent for the 2027 China–South Asia Expo ([EconomyNext](https://economynext.com/sri-lanka-chinas-yunnan-sign-three-free-three-facilitation-to-boost-trade-287734)).
