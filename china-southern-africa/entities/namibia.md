# Namibia

Namibia is extending China's Southern African footprint beyond the Copperbelt. It holds uranium, lithium and rare-earth deposits, an Atlantic coastline and a stable legal system, and the government wants Chinese capital for processing rather than more raw exports. President Nandi-Ndaitwah made Beijing her first state visit outside Africa in July 2026.

## Key Actions

- **2024-2026:** Namibia restricted exports of unprocessed lithium, cobalt, graphite, manganese and rare earths without written approval.
- **July 2026:** Nandi-Ndaitwah's Beijing visit produced eight agreements, including a framework economic partnership and green-minerals cooperation.
- **July 2026:** The joint statement committed both sides to local processing, technology transfer and skills development in uranium, lithium and rare earths.
- **July 2026:** Chinalco moved to acquire a 95% stake in Celsius Resources' Opuwo cobalt-copper project, committing N$16.5 million for exploration.

## Current Position

Namibia has converted its mineral endowment into leverage. The agreements are broader than a single mine: they cover processing, energy, agriculture and logistics, and China General Nuclear already operates the Husab uranium mine and partners on desalination. The government presents Namibia as a low-risk entry point for Chinese firms that face tougher conditions in the DRC and Zimbabwe.

## Constraints

- Water and power are scarce, which limits how much processing can be sited locally.
- The population is small, so the domestic market cannot absorb much of the output.
- Export restrictions apply mainly to new projects, leaving existing licences largely untouched.
