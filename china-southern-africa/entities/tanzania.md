# Tanzania

Tanzania is the Indian Ocean outlet for Copperbelt minerals and is trying to become a processing hub rather than a transit corridor. About 70% of DRC exports to China pass through Dar es Salaam, and the TAZARA railway gives Zambia an alternative to South African ports. The government wants Chinese capital for plants and railways while keeping space for Western investment in nickel.

## Key Actions

- **May 2026:** A Chinese firm confirmed neodymium and praseodymium deposits in the Njombe region.
- **August 2026:** A Chinese contractor won a $2.7 billion railway project, alongside the TAZARA upgrade and a spur to a Burundi nickel mine.
- **September 2026:** Shenghe Resources began building a heavy-minerals separation plant at Pangani to process feedstock from Mozambique, South Africa and Madagascar.
- **September 2026:** Tanzania and the China Geological Survey agreed on grant-funded airborne surveys and exploration training.

## Current Position

Tanzania is converting its position as a gateway into an industrial policy. The Shenghe plant is the first Chinese processing hub designed to serve projects across several countries, and it fits the government's beneficiation requirements. At the same time, a US-backed consortium has been negotiating a stake in the Kabanga nickel project, so Tanzania is not relying on one partner.

## Constraints

- Port and rail capacity remain below what Copperbelt exporters need.
- The TAZARA line is old and its rehabilitation is incomplete.
- Processing plants need reliable power that the grid does not yet guarantee.
