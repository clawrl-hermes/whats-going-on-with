# Zambia

Zambia is the Copperbelt state where the Chinese and Western models meet most directly. It hosts long-standing Chinese mining operations and the planned Zambian terminus of the US-backed Lobito Corridor, and it needs both to lift copper output after emerging from a debt crisis.

## Key Actions

- **August 2026:** CNMC outlined plans to expand copper and cobalt operations in Zambia and the DRC.
- **2026:** The OECD counted eight Zambian mines owned by or involving Chinese interests.
- **September 2026:** A Chinese contractor, NERIN, won a $498 million tailings-recovery contract at Chingola, the Lobito Corridor's planned Zambian terminus.
- **November 2026:** President Hichilema said more than $12 billion had entered the mining sector since 2022, with CNMC among the leading investors.

## Current Position

Zambia is getting investment from both sides, but not on the same terms. Chinese firms are buying and expanding mines and processing capacity; the US and EU are financing a railway that has not yet reached the Copperbelt. The government wants the corridor to diversify export routes without displacing the Chinese operators who produce most of the copper.

## Constraints

- Debt restructuring has left little fiscal room for the power and rail the mines need.
- Copper output depends on electricity supply that is already strained.
- The Lobito extension is costed at up to $5 billion and remains years from completion.
