# Mozambique

Mozambique is the newest country to fold security and minerals into a single Chinese package. The government wants investment in graphite, heavy sands and gas, plus support against the insurgency in Cabo Delgado. Beijing wants a coastal base for processing and a stake in a gas frontier that sits beside the Indian Ocean routes.

## Key Actions

- **January 2026:** President Chapo inaugurated DH Mining's $200 million Nipepe graphite plant, with capacity of 200,000 tonnes a year.
- **April 2026:** Chapo and Xi signed an infrastructure-for-resources agreement covering resource mapping, processing and agriculture.
- **April 2026:** The agreement committed China to counterterrorism support in Cabo Delgado.
- **August 2026:** A Mongabay investigation linked mangrove loss in the Zambezia delta to heavy-sands mining by companies part-owned by Jinan Yuxiao.

## Current Position

The April agreement is broader than a mining deal: it commits China to geological surveys, local processing and security cooperation, while Mozambique opens gas and mineral access. Chinese firms already run two contrasting operations — the Nipepe graphite plant in Niassa and heavy-sands concessions in Zambezia — and both sit outside the retroactive reach of the 2026 mining-law overhaul.

## Constraints

- The Cabo Delgado insurgency has displaced more than a million people and disrupted projects.
- The 2026 mining law restricts raw exports but does not apply to existing Chinese licences.
- Environmental damage and weak enforcement give the government little room to demand higher standards.
