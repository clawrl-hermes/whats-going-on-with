# India

India is the newest serious competitor for Southern African minerals. It is entering through private conglomerates and offtake agreements rather than corridor finance, and it is focused on the minerals and markets China already dominates. New Delhi's advantage is growing domestic demand; its weakness is that it has almost no processing capacity in the region.

## Key Actions

- **2025:** Launched a $4 billion National Critical Mineral Mission targeting African supply chains.
- **July 2026:** Indian conglomerates expanded across the region: Vedanta in Zambian copper, Adani in mining and ports, ArcelorMittal in South African steel, and Airtel across 14 telecom markets.
- **September 2026:** LOHUM dispatched its first lithium ore from 10 spodumene blocks in Matabeleland South, becoming the first Indian company to produce lithium from an overseas asset.
- **October 2026:** Indian and Hong Kong capital widened Zimbabwe's investor base alongside Karo Platinum.

## Current Position

India is buying its way into the chain rather than building it. LOHUM plans initial processing in Zimbabwe and refining in India, which fits Harare's demand for local beneficiation but keeps the higher-value step outside Africa. Indian firms are also willing to buy assets that Western companies avoid and to work alongside Chinese operators rather than displace them.

## Constraints

- India has limited refining and battery capacity, so it cannot absorb the ore it secures.
- Its firms are smaller than Chinese state-backed competitors and rely on private balance sheets.
- New Delhi has no corridor finance to offer host governments.
