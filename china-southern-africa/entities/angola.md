# Angola

Angola controls the Atlantic end of the Lobito Corridor and is the corridor state with the most to gain from it. The government wants Chinese and Western money to build port and rail capacity that turns transit into a substitute for declining oil revenue, while keeping both sides competing for access.

## Key Actions

- **July 2026:** Secured $900 million of private Chinese investment for the Barra do Dande port terminal under a 25-year build-operate agreement.
- **September 2026:** The DRC awarded Mota-Engil a 30-year concession for the Dilolo-Sakania line, the Congolese section feeding Angola's railway.
- **October 2026:** The AFC named Mota-Engil and Odebrecht for the Angolan section as part of the wider Lobito build-out.
- **November 2026:** The DRC-US joint committee reviewed the Dilolo-Sakania link, the section that connects the Copperbelt to Angola's Atlantic line.

## Current Position

Angola sits between two financing blocs and benefits from both. China built and refurbished the railway and is now funding a new port; the US and EU are funding the corridor upgrades that give the line its strategic value. Luanda has not chosen between them, and the transit fees are the same whichever flag the copper ships under.

## Constraints

- Oil still supplies most government revenue, so transit income cannot replace it quickly.
- The railway's capacity is limited by decades of neglect and by flooding on the western sections.
- Chinese loans from the oil-for-infrastructure era constrain how much new debt Luanda can take on.
