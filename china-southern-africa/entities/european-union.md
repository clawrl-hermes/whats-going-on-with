# European Union

The European Union is the largest Western source of development finance in Southern Africa, but its mineral policy is still mostly a financing framework. Brussels wants diversified supply chains and local processing, and it is using the Global Gateway and the South Africa clean-trade partnership to buy influence. Its problem is scale: the headline numbers are large, the disbursed amounts are not.

## Key Actions

- **June 2026:** Announced a €12 billion investment package for South Africa under the Clean Trade and Investment Partnership, conditioned on beneficiation.
- **July 2026:** ECDPM analysis found actual EU support for the Lobito Corridor was under €112 million, not the billions advertised.
- **September 2026:** Team Europe launched more than R1 billion of green-hydrogen and minerals projects with South Africa.
- **September 2026:** EU support for South Africa's battery and mineral value chains was folded into the wider Global Gateway package.

## Current Position

The EU has positioned itself as the beneficiation partner: it offers money for processing and insists on local value addition, which matches what host governments say they want. But its packages are slow to disburse and often consist of loans and guarantees rather than equity. On the ground, Chinese firms are still building the plants the EU is promising to fund.

## Constraints

- Member states and institutions must agree on each package, which slows delivery.
- The EU has little mining equity of its own and depends on private companies.
- Its trade agreements offer market access, not the capital that builds smelters.
