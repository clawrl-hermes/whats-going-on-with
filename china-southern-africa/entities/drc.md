# Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC)

The DRC is the core of China's Southern African mineral position and the most important test of its processing model. It produces about 73% of the world's mined cobalt and 3.2 million tonnes of copper a year, and Chinese firms hold stakes in 54% of its critical mining projects. The government wants to convert that leverage into smelters and refineries, and it is using export controls to force the issue.

## Key Actions

- **February 2025:** The DRC banned raw cobalt exports, replacing the ban with a quota system in September 2025.
- **May 2026:** Zijin's Manono lithium project began production, a month ahead of schedule.
- **June 2026:** A joint ministerial order banned copper and cobalt concentrate exports, allowing one-year strategic waivers; Kamoa-Kakula's smelter, Africa's largest, began operating.
- **September 2026:** The DRC and the United States created a task force to accelerate mining, energy and processing projects.
- **October 2026:** The EY/Africa Business+ database put Chinese stakes in 54% of critical mining projects, up from under a third in 2023.

## Current Position

The DRC has more pricing power than any other producer and is using it. Cobalt controls lifted prices above $58,000 a tonne in early 2026, and the concentrate ban is meant to push copper and cobalt refining onshore. But the same Chinese companies that own the mines are building the plants, so the ban has not changed ownership. The US partnership is the government's attempt to bring in a second source of capital.

## Constraints

- Mining faces a power deficit of about 1 GW, and Kamoa-Kakula's demand is still rising.
- The rail network is fragmented, and the Lobito and Dilolo-Sakania links are years from full capacity.
- Battery makers are shifting to lower-cobalt chemistries, which erodes the value of the DRC's main lever.
