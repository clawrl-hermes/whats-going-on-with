# China

China is the dominant external actor in Southern Africa's mineral economy. It is the largest trading partner, the largest source of infrastructure finance and the owner or operator of a large share of the region's mines and processing plants. Beijing's objective is not simply to buy ore but to control the chain from mine to refined material, and to keep that position as host governments demand more local processing.

## Key Actions

- **May 2026:** Granted zero-tariff treatment to 53 African countries.
- **July 2026:** Signed eight agreements with Namibia covering uranium, lithium and rare-earth processing.
- **July 2026:** Chinese BRI investment in Africa reached $33.5 billion in the first half of 2026, up 254%.
- **September 2026:** Shenghe began a heavy-minerals plant at Pangani, Tanzania, designed to process feedstock from several countries.
- **October 2026:** Elevated Botswana to a comprehensive strategic partnership; Xi told President Boko that "Africa's resources belong to Africa".
- **November 2026:** BRI spending on metals and mining reached a record $32 billion for 2025.

## Current Position

China has adapted faster than its rivals to the region's export bans. Instead of resisting beneficiation, Chinese firms built the sulphate and smelting capacity that Zimbabwe and the DRC demanded, which kept the ore flowing and the processing margin Chinese. The model is now under pressure from Western corridor finance and from governments that want ownership, not just plants.

## Constraints

- Export bans force capital into processing that would otherwise have stayed in China.
- Power and rail deficits in the DRC and Zimbabwe can delay projects after they are announced.
- Western counter-finance and India's entry give host governments alternative partners.
