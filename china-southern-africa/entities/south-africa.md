# South Africa

South Africa is China's largest African trading partner and the region's most industrialised economy, which gives it unusual leverage and an unusually large trade deficit. Pretoria wants Chinese money for its grid and Chinese factories for its industrial base, but it also wants to stop exporting unprocessed ore. That contradiction defines the relationship.

## Key Actions

- **May 2026:** South Africa opened its freight rail network to private operators, the biggest rail reform in decades.
- **June 2026:** The EU announced a €12 billion investment package tied to beneficiation.
- **August 2026:** A delegation led by Electricity Minister Ramokgopa pitched R2.2 trillion of energy investment to Chinese firms in Beijing.
- **September 2026:** Three Chinese manufacturers confirmed transformer, wire and pylon plants in South Africa.
- **September 2026:** Team Europe launched more than R1 billion of green-hydrogen and minerals projects.

## Current Position

South Africa is being courted by both blocs and is trying to convert that competition into industrial capacity. China is the larger trade partner and the faster supplier of grid equipment. The EU package is smaller but explicitly conditions support on local processing. Neither has closed the structural gap: gold, platinum and chrome still dominate exports, while machinery and batteries dominate imports.

## Constraints

- The trade relationship is lopsided: roughly $13 billion of exports against $23 billion of imports in 2025.
- Power shortages and rail bottlenecks limit how quickly new plants can operate.
- US tariffs and aid cuts have narrowed South Africa's Western alternatives, weakening its bargaining position.
