This topic tracks China's infrastructure and mineral strategy across Southern Africa: the rail corridors, ports and processing plants built to move copper, cobalt, lithium, graphite and platinum from the Copperbelt and Zimbabwe to global markets.

The through-line is the shift from extraction to processing. African governments have raised export barriers and demanded local beneficiation, and Chinese firms have responded by building smelters, sulphate plants and industrial parks — often faster than Western alternatives. The result is a deepening interdependence that Western de-risking efforts have struggled to reverse.

Coverage follows the Lobito and TAZARA corridors, the ownership of mines and ports, the US, EU, Quad and Indian counter-moves, and the governance and human-rights questions raised by the pace of Chinese investment.
