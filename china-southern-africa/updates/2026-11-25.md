- **Kinshasa shuts cobalt exports again, reversing its own quota system** — ARECOMS suspended all cobalt exports from 30 November until further notice, covering industrial, semi-industrial and artisanal output, after replacing its first ban with a 96,600-tonne quota for 2026. It cited a price decline since June, rising inventories and speculation; the DRC shipped 61,441 tonnes in the first nine months of 2026 against more than 145,000 tonnes in the same period of 2024, and a four-month review will not automatically restore shipments ([MINING.COM](https://www.mining.com/web/congo-halts-cobalt-exports-again-to-tackle-oversupply-support-prices-industry-regulator-says)).
- **Chinese cobalt producers get a one-week window to clear stock** — CMOC, the world's largest mined cobalt producer and holder of one of the largest DRC quotas, said it would expedite cargoes before the deadline, with some already en route. Because Chinese operators hold the biggest allocations, the suspension freezes their volumes first, and unshipped allocations do not carry over ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/economy/global-economy/article/3375611/chinese-cobalt-firms-race-beat-dr-congos-export-ban-key-battery-material)).
- **Zimbabwe shifts the lithium export tax onto sulphate value, closing the raw-export loophole** — In the 2027 budget, Finance Minister Mthuli Ncube said 6% concentrate converts at five tonnes to one tonne of sulphate and is taxed on the gross fair market value of that sulphate, with sulphate exports still zero-rated and transport costs not deductible. The budget also obliges miners and large businesses to source 15% of inputs locally; Ncube did not say whether the January 2027 concentrate ban stands ([MINING.COM](https://www.mining.com/web/zimbabwe-tweaks-lithium-export-tax-rule-mandates-local-sourcing-by-miners), [SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/K62NN6D5FA6I4QMSPRRTIRAHSLHPV2BT6TGVITQ-smm-analysiszimbabwe-shifts-lithium-export-tax-to-sulphate-value)).
- **Zambia puts €60m into the Copperbelt line and dates the Lobito railway to late 2027** — The 2027 budget funds rehabilitation of the Kapiri Mposhi–Ndola section, makes Zambia Railways the infrastructure manager and opens freight to private operators on an open-access model, repealing the 1984 Railways Act. Musokotwane said Lobito construction would begin in the fourth quarter of 2027, with TAZARA works continuing and a dry port planned at Kapiri Mposhi ([Zambia Monitor](https://www.zambiamonitor.com/zambia-secures-initial-e60-million-for-railway-rehabilitation)).
- **Zambia makes a Chinese-owned processor finish its clean-up before it expands** — Environment Minister Gilbert Siame directed ZEMA to enforce restoration of the Dambo wetland and Luela Stream at Rongxing's Kalulushi plant and to fine the company for unauthorised construction, while Rongxing sought approval to double copper processing capacity. Government put K16.2 million in compensation to 507 farmers affected by Rongxing and Sino-Metals pollution behind the 2025 Kafue spill ([Zambia Monitor](https://www.zambiamonitor.com/zambia-orders-rongxing-to-complete-environmental-clean-up-at-kalulushi-plant)).
- **Lobito's operator doubles volumes but still moves a fraction of the Copperbelt's copper** — Lobito Atlantic Railway's chief operating officer Nicholas Gregoir said freight has more than doubled since January 2024, with about 200,000 tonnes of copper expected in 2026 and a 12,000-tonne cathode train shipped mostly to the US. Angola's foreign minister Tete Antonio said exporting raw material exports jobs and knowledge, the same complaint Harare and Kinshasa have turned into export bans ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cx053jrv9rz9o)).
