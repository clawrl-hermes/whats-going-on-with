# Timeline: Chinese Infrastructure and Minerals in Southern Africa

## 2019
- Chinese state-backed firms hold stakes across the Copperbelt and Zimbabwe, with BRI-era lending the main channel for infrastructure.

## 2022
- China accounts for roughly 70% of DRC mineral exports, most routed through Dar es Salaam, and Chinese firms dominate cobalt processing.

## 2023
- Zimbabwe begins signalling a ban on raw lithium concentrate exports and demands domestic processing.

## 2024
- Zimbabwe bans raw lithium exports, prompting Chinese miners to commit processing investment.
- The Lobito Corridor gains momentum as a 1,600km rail route from the Copperbelt to Angola's Atlantic port.

## 2025
- Chinese lithium miners invest about $1 billion in Zimbabwean sulphate plants.
- China becomes Africa's largest trading partner, with bilateral trade above $300 billion.

## 2026
- **January:**
  - DH Mining, a Jinan Yuxiao subsidiary, opens a $200 million graphite plant at Nipepe, Mozambique.
- **April:**
  - Zimbabwe ships its first lithium sulphate from Arcadia; Chengxin and Sinomine receive export quotas.
- **May:**
  - China grants zero-tariff treatment on 100% of tariff lines to 53 African countries.
  - A Chinese firm confirms neodymium and praseodymium deposits in Tanzania's Njombe region.
  - AidData puts Chinese development finance to the DRC at $22 billion across 112 loans from 2000 to 2023.
- **June:**
  - The EU launches a €12 billion investment package in South Africa, with beneficiation as a condition.
  - Kamoa-Kakula's copper smelter, Africa's largest, becomes operational in the DRC.
  - Zimbabwe opens talks with China Railway on resource-backed infrastructure modelled on Sicomines.
  - The G7 adopts a critical minerals action plan, and the US secures a preliminary deal for Kenya's Mrima Hill.
- **July:**
  - Namibia's president visits Beijing; eight agreements cover uranium, lithium and rare-earth processing.
  - South Africa opens its freight rail network to private operators.
  - Glencore sells 40% stakes in DRC copper-cobalt assets to a US-Gulf consortium.
  - Angola secures $900 million in Chinese private investment for the Barra do Dande port terminal.
  - Mutapa confirms $300 million from Huayou and Tsingshan for the Sandawana lithium project.
  - Zimbabwe designates 13 more minerals for a raw-export ban from 2027.
  - Chinese BRI investment in Africa jumps 254% to $33.5 billion in the first half of the year.
- **August:**
  - South Africa pitches R2.2 trillion ($122 billion) of energy investment to Chinese firms in Beijing.
  - A CRS report finds Africa supplied 99.9% of China's cobalt imports and 86% of its natural graphite in 2025.
  - Zimbabwe's first-half lithium exports rise 230% to $782 million.
  - Chinalco moves into Namibian copper-cobalt, and a Chinese contractor wins a $2.7 billion Tanzanian railway.
- **September:**
  - The DRC bans copper and cobalt concentrate exports, allowing only one-year strategic waivers.
  - Sinomine secures another 300,000-tonne Zimbabwean quota; announced Chinese lithium investment reaches $2.8 billion.
  - Shenghe begins a heavy-minerals separation plant in Pangani, Tanzania.
  - China NERIN wins a $498 million copper tailings contract at Chingola, on the Lobito corridor's Zambian terminus.
  - CEDESA argues China effectively controls a key segment of the Western-backed Lobito Corridor.
  - Zimbabwe's lithium export earnings reach $2.16 billion in nine months, nearly four times 2025.
  - A human rights report records 100 abuse allegations at Chinese mines in Africa, up from 45.
- **October:**
  - Chinese firms hold stakes in 54% of the DRC's critical mining projects, up from under a third in 2023.
  - African mineral processing facilities rise 28% in a year, with Chinese groups involved in 15.
  - Tsingshan's Dinson signs a rail partnership with Zimbabwe's NRZ to move coal and steel.
  - Botswana is elevated to a comprehensive strategic partnership during President Boko's Beijing visit.
  - The OECD documents Western stockpiling and midstream initiatives; the AFC names preferred bidders for Zambia's Lobito section.
- **November:**
  - Foreign Affairs reports China spent a record $32 billion on BRI metals and mining in 2025.
  - A proposed Angola-Tanzania railway would link the Atlantic and Indian Ocean corridors (planned).
