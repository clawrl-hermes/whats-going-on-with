This topic tracks Bolivia's political crisis under President Rodrigo Paz, from the collapse of the MAS party and the 2025 election through the fuel-subsidy shock, the blockade wave and the IMF programme that followed.

The through-line is a government with a weak electoral mandate trying to stabilise an economy short of dollars, fuel and gas revenue. Paz has governed through decrees, a state of exception and an unstable centre-right coalition, while Evo Morales remains a fugitive opposition figure and the MAS vote has fragmented.

Coverage also follows the external alignments shaping the crisis: US security and financial support, Chinese and Russian lithium contracts, the regional rightward shift, and Bolivia's place in a contest over critical minerals and hemispheric influence.
