# United States

The Trump administration is the closest external ally Rodrigo Paz has. Washington provides balance-of-payments support through the IMF, security cooperation against drug trafficking and the Morales network, and a critical-minerals opening that could reduce Bolivia's dependence on Chinese and Russian capital. In return it gets a partner in the Shield of the Americas and a government that aligns with its anti-narcotics agenda.

## Key Actions

- **June 2026:** Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth publicly backed Paz during the blockade crisis.
- **July 2026:** A $20 million train-and-equip agreement was signed, the DEA reopened its La Paz office, and Bolivia joined the Shield of the Americas.
- **September 2026:** Rubio met Paz at the UN General Assembly and called Bolivia a "critically important regional partner".
- **October 2026:** Washington revoked Attorney General Roger Mariaca's visa; he was arrested two days later, and the State Department named 15 Bolivians under visa restrictions.
- **November 2026:** The administration nominated a new ambassador, filling a post empty for years.

## Current Position

US-Bolivia relations are the warmest in decades. Washington treats Paz as a stabilisation partner and a counterweight to Chinese and Russian influence, and it has used visa revocations and drug investigations to reshape Bolivia's justice system. The relationship gives Paz external legitimacy, but it also makes him dependent on a patron whose attention can move elsewhere.

## Constraints

- US support is conditional and comes with human-rights criticism that limits how far Paz can rely on it domestically.
- The relationship is personal to the Trump administration, not institutionalised in either country's bureaucracy.
- Washington cannot replace the multilateral financing Bolivia needs, and it will not fill the Chinese investment gap in lithium.
