# Evo Morales

Morales governed Bolivia for nearly fourteen years and remains the most recognisable figure of the Bolivian left. He is now a fugitive in the Chapare, guarded by cocaleros and facing three separate legal fronts, and his ability to set the political agenda is declining faster than his visibility.

## Key Actions

- **November 2019:** Resigned and left the country after the contested election and the police and military withdrawal of support.
- **2025:** Returned to the Chapare and became the centre of opposition to the Arce government, while facing a trafficking warrant.
- **July 2026:** Prosecutors issued a second warrant, for armed insurrection and terrorism over the blockade wave.
- **September 2026:** Convened the "Evo Pueblo" assembly, gave Paz until 3 October to repeal the diesel decrees, and threatened national protests.
- **October 2026:** Faced a third investigation, over the 2009 Las Américas hotel killings, and warned that "at any moment armed struggle can return" while saying he opposed it.

## Current Position

Morales can still convene a congress and set a date, but he cannot deliver a national mobilization. The October deadline passed without the predicted mass protest, the COB refused to align with his movement, and the evista march was postponed repeatedly. His base is the Chapare cocaleros, not the broader labour and indigenous movement that once made MAS unbeatable.

## Constraints

- Three active legal fronts keep him confined to the Chapare and limit his travel and bargaining power.
- He has no party vehicle: MAS is split and holds only two seats in the lower house.
- His association with the 2026 crisis has reinforced the perception that he represents the exhaustion of the MAS era, not its renewal.
