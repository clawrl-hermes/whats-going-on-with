# International Monetary Fund

The IMF is the external anchor of Rodrigo Paz's economic programme. Its $1.9 billion, 36-month Extended Fund Facility is Bolivia's first multi-year arrangement since 2006, and its conditions — ending fuel subsidies, fiscal consolidation and a ban on central-bank financing — have become the government's domestic political agenda. The Fund does not implement policy, but it decides which policies are fundable.

## Key Actions

- **July 2026:** Reached a staff-level agreement on a $1.9 billion EFF, less than half the $3.3 billion Bolivia had requested.
- **September 2026:** Congress approved the programme after the government disclosed that it required all fuel subsidies to end by 2027.
- **October 2026:** The Executive Board approved the facility on 2 October, releasing $214 million immediately.
- **October 2026:** The WEO projected Bolivia as South America's only economy contracting through 2027, at about -3.0%.

## Current Position

The programme has given Paz a credible fiscal anchor and unlocked roughly $4-5 billion in additional multilateral financing. It has also locked in the confrontation: the subsidy phase-out is the measure that produced the May-June blockades, and the 2027 budget must cut spending to a ceiling the Assembly has not yet accepted. The Fund's reviews will become referendums on the government's survival.

## Constraints

- Its conditions are politically explosive in a country where fuel prices are the central social contract.
- The facility is too small to cover the financing gap on its own and depends on the World Bank, IDB and CAF delivering.
- It has no enforcement power beyond disbursement decisions, so slippage shows up as a funding crisis rather than a formal breach.
