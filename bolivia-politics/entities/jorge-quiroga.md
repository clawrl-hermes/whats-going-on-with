# Jorge Quiroga

Quiroga is the elder figure of Bolivia's traditional right and the candidate Rodrigo Paz defeated in the 2025 runoff. He holds no office and commands no party machine, but he remains a reference point for the conservative voters Paz needs and a reminder that the right that won in 2025 is not the right that governed in the 1990s.

## Role

Quiroga was president from 2001 to 2002 and later led the orthodox, market-reform wing of Bolivian politics. His administration was the last before MAS took power, which makes him both the right's elder statesman and a reminder of the elite voters rejected in 2005. In 2025 he ran as the conservative alternative to MAS and reached the runoff, where Paz's centrist coalition defeated him. His voters became part of Paz's governing majority even though Quiroga himself stayed outside the administration.

## Current Position

He has no formal role in the government or the IMF programme. His influence is indirect: he legitimises the right's turn to stabilisation and gives conservative critics a figure to rally around when Paz's decree-based government looks improvisational. He is a barometer of the traditional right's patience, not a decision-maker.

## Constraints

- He has no congressional bloc and no party structure of his own.
- He is associated with the pre-MAS political elite that voters punished in 2025.
- The coalition that backed his runoff campaign did not survive into government.
