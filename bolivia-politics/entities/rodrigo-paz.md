# Rodrigo Paz

Paz is Bolivia's first right-of-centre president in two decades and the least ideological leader in South America's conservative turn. He came to office promising to stabilise an economy short of dollars and fuel, and his presidency has become a test of whether an IMF programme can be implemented by a leader without a legislative majority or a party.

## Key Actions

- **November 2025:** Took office after defeating Jorge Quiroga in the runoff, inheriting fuel shortages, depleted reserves and rising inflation.
- **June 2026:** Ended the 15-year dollar peg and declared a state of emergency to clear the blockade wave.
- **July 2026:** Reached a staff-level agreement with the IMF on a $1.9 billion, 36-month Extended Fund Facility.
- **September 2026:** Congress approved the IMF programme; Paz signed the decree ending the diesel subsidy hours later, triggering the "dieselazo".
- **October 2026:** Secured the IMF Board's approval and a Moody's upgrade, hosted Chile's first state visit in 71 years, and declared an El Niño emergency.

## Current Position

Paz has stabilised the macroeconomy on paper and isolated himself politically. Reserves are recovering, the fiscal balance has improved, and the IMF programme is funded, but only six of his original fifteen ministers remain, the National Unity party has left the coalition, and Congress cannot organise its committees. He governs by decree because he cannot govern through the Assembly, and each decree produces the protest wave it is meant to pre-empt.

## Constraints

- His bloc holds 65 of 130 seats; the coalition is six centre-right groupings with no shared programme.
- The fuel-subsidy phase-out is an IMF condition that guarantees continued social conflict.
- His authority depends on US support and military deployment, not on domestic consensus.
