Bolivia is a year into Rodrigo Paz's presidency and still governing from crisis to crisis. The IMF approved a $1.9 billion facility on 2 October and disbursed $214 million immediately, but the diesel subsidy's 83% price rise triggered the dieselazo, and the government has since arrested the attorney general and opened a third legal front against Evo Morales. The labour movement has not called a general strike and the opposition remains fragmented, which has kept Paz in office without giving him a stable majority. The economy is still contracting, with the IMF projecting a 3% contraction for 2026 even as reserves are expected to rise, and a nationwide El Niño emergency now sits on top of the political one.

## The Open Question

Can Paz turn IMF stabilisation and a fragmented opposition into durable authority before the petrol subsidy phase-out and the El Niño emergency converge?
