# Bolivia's Post-MAS Fragmentation

## Overview

Bolivia's post-MAS politics is more fragmented than the party system it replaced. MAS governed for nearly two decades through a corporate relationship with social movements, and its collapse left no institution capable of aggregating those movements into a governing majority. Paz won the presidency with a coalition that had no party structure, no shared ideology and no legislative base, and he has governed against a Congress of six centre-right blocs and a street that no longer maps onto left and right.

## The Vacuum

MAS destroyed itself before the 2025 election through a three-way fight between Morales, Luis Arce and Andrónico Rodríguez. The party that could turn protests into votes lost that capacity, and the 2026 protest wave failed partly because Morales embodied exhaustion rather than renewal. The left returned to its traditional fragmentation, with no leader able to unite cocaleros, unions and urban progressives. The right inherited power without inheriting a party.

## Three Levels of Fracture

The fragmentation runs through three layers. In the legislature, Paz's bloc holds 65 of 130 seats and depends on allies who have left over his use of decrees. Inside the executive, the PDC is split between Paz and Vice-President Edmand Lara, and only six of the original fifteen ministers remain. On the street, the opposition is split between evistas, the COB, teachers, regional civic committees and Indigenous organisations, each with its own demands and no common programme.

## Governing by Parallel Tracks

Because no single channel works, the government runs several at once: decrees and emergency powers to clear roads; multilateral financing through the IMF and IDB to stabilise the economy; a purge of prosecutors and judges to break criminal networks; and security cooperation with Washington through the Shield of the Americas. Each track consumes capacity, and none builds durable consent. The result is a government that can pass measures but cannot make them legitimate.

## Current Status

The IMF programme has given the government an economic anchor, and the opposition has failed to convert discontent into a national challenge. But the coalition is thinner than when Paz took office, the Assembly cannot organise its committees, and the 2027 budget will test whether decree government can survive a year of adjustment. The post-MAS vacuum has not been filled by a new political order; it has been filled by ad hoc crisis management.
